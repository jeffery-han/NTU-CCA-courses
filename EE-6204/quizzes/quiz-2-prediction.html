Q1 — Random variables and Poisson processes 7

A help desk receives calls on two independent lines. Calls on line A form a Poisson process with rate 3 per hour, and calls on line B form a Poisson process with rate 2 per hour.

Q2 — Inventory model 6

A shop sells a spare part. Weekly demands \(D_1, D_2, \ldots\) are independent Poisson random variables with mean 0.5. Let \(X_n\) be the number of parts in stock at the end of week \(n\), with \(0 \le X_0 \le 2\). If the stock at the end of a week is 0, the shop orders 2 parts, which arrive before the next week opens; otherwise no order is placed. Demand that cannot be met is lost. Model \(\{X_n\}\) as a DTMC on \(S = \{0, 1, 2\}\).

Q3 — Machine with failures 5

A machine repeatedly performs a setup (state 0, duration EXP(\(s\))) and then processes one part (state 1, duration EXP(\(p\))), after which the next setup begins. The machine can fail during setup and during processing; the time to failure is EXP(\(f\)) in either state. A failed machine (state 2) is repaired in EXP(\(r\)) time; after repair the unfinished part is discarded and a fresh setup starts. All durations are independent. Rates (per hour): \(s = 6,\ p = 3,\ f = 0.5,\ r = 2\). Model the system as a CTMC on \(\{0, 1, 2\}\).

Q4 — Choosing a print-room design 6

Jobs arrive at a print room as a Poisson process at 9 per hour. Service times are exponential. Use the exam appendix where helpful.
