0 · The map: what each stage produces and why the next stage needs it

A digital controller only sees the plant at the sampling instants \(t=kT\). So the job is to turn a continuous model into a discrete one, check that it can be controlled at all, and then pick a gain. Each stage feeds the next:

StageInputOutputWhy the next stage needs it
1 Modelphysics / diagram\(\mathbf A,\mathbf B,\mathbf C\), \(G(s)\)\(\mathbf A\) governs how the state evolves
2 Transition matrix\(\mathbf A\)\(\mathbf\Phi(t)=e^{\mathbf At}\)it moves the state across one period \(T\)
3 ZOH discretisation\(\mathbf\Phi(t),\mathbf B,T\)\(\mathbf\Phi(T),\mathbf\Theta(T)\)every discrete test and design uses these two
4 Structural checks\(\mathbf\Phi,\mathbf\Theta,\mathbf C\)\(\mathbf W_C\), \(\mathbf W_O\)if \(\mathbf W_C\) is singular, no \(\mathbf K\) exists
5 Discrete TF\(\mathbf\Phi,\mathbf\Theta,\mathbf C\)\(G(z)\), open-loop polesshows what the controller must move

7 Verify\(\mathbf\Phi-\mathbf\Theta\mathbf K\), \(\mathbf x(0)\)\(\mathbf x(k)\), \(u(k)\)proves the design does what was asked

Conventions (LPH Lect 1–3). \(\mathbf x(k+1)=\mathbf\Phi\mathbf x(k)+\mathbf\Theta u(k)\), \(y(k)=\mathbf C\mathbf x(k)\), state feedback \(u(k)=-\mathbf K\mathbf x(k)\). Bold = matrix or vector; \(n=2\) states throughout.

The plant. \[\dot{\mathbf x}=\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}-1&0\\1&-2\end{bmatrix}}_{\mathbf A}\mathbf x+\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}1\\0\end{bmatrix}}_{\mathbf B}u,\qquad y=\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}0&1\end{bmatrix}}_{\mathbf C}\mathbf x,\qquad T=0.5\text{ s, ZOH}.\] Goal: a digital state-feedback gain that drives any initial state to zero as fast as possible (deadbeat), checked from \(\mathbf x(0)=[1\ \ 1]^{\mathsf T}\).

1 · Read the model and its transfer function \(G(s)\)

Write \(\dot{\mathbf x}=\mathbf A\mathbf x+\mathbf Bu\) out row by row. Each row is one first-order ODE:

\[\dot x_1=-x_1+u,\qquad \dot x_2=x_1-2x_2.\]

So \(u\) pushes \(x_1\), and \(x_1\) in turn pushes \(x_2\). It is a chain of two first-order lags, and we measure the end of the chain.

Step: \(G(s)=\mathbf C(s\mathbf I-\mathbf A)^{-1}\mathbf B\)

\(s\mathbf I-\mathbf A=\begin{bmatrix}s+1&0\\-1&s+2\end{bmatrix}\), \(\det=(s+1)(s+2)\). The \(2\times2\) inverse is the adjugate (swap the diagonal, negate the off-diagonal) over the determinant:

\[(s\mathbf I-\mathbf A)^{-1}=\frac{1}{(s+1)(s+2)}\begin{bmatrix}s+2&0\\1&s+1\end{bmatrix}.\]

\(\mathbf C=[0\ 1]\) keeps row 2; \(\mathbf B=[1\ 0]^{\mathsf T}\) keeps column 1. The (2,1) entry of the adjugate is \(1\), so

\[G(s)=\frac{1}{(s+1)(s+2)},\qquad \text{poles } s=-1,\,-2\ (=\text{eigenvalues of }\mathbf A),\qquad G(0)=\tfrac12.\]

Block-diagram check: \(\frac1{s+1}\) feeding \(\frac1{s+2}\) in series gives the same product. ✓ Deeper walkthrough: transfer-function explainer.

Pitfall. \(\mathbf A\) is lower-triangular, so its eigenvalues are simply the diagonal entries \(-1,-2\). Don't expand a determinant you can read off.

Check: DC gain

Hold \(u=1\) forever. What value does \(y\) settle to? (Hint: \(G(0)\) from \(G(s)=\frac1{(s+1)(s+2)}\).)

2 · The state transition matrix \(\mathbf\Phi(t)=e^{\mathbf At}\)

What it is. With \(u=0\), the solution of \(\dot{\mathbf x}=\mathbf A\mathbf x\) is \(\mathbf x(t)=\mathbf\Phi(t)\mathbf x(0)\). So \(\mathbf\Phi(t)\) is the matrix that "moves the state forward by time \(t\)". LPH computes it as the inverse Laplace transform of the resolvent (Lect 1):

\[\mathbf\Phi(t)=\mathcal L^{-1}\big\{(s\mathbf I-\mathbf A)^{-1}\big\}.\]

Why this works: taking Laplace transforms of \(\dot{\mathbf x}=\mathbf A\mathbf x\) gives \(s\mathbf X-\mathbf x(0)=\mathbf A\mathbf X\), so \(\mathbf X(s)=(s\mathbf I-\mathbf A)^{-1}\mathbf x(0)\).

Step 1: partial fractions for every entry

Reuse the resolvent from Stage 1:

\[(s\mathbf I-\mathbf A)^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}\dfrac1{s+1}&0\\[2mm]\dfrac1{(s+1)(s+2)}&\dfrac1{s+2}\end{bmatrix},\qquad \frac1{(s+1)(s+2)}=\frac1{s+1}-\frac1{s+2}.\]

The cover-up rule gives this: at \(s=-1\) the residue is \(\frac1{-1+2}=1\), and at \(s=-2\) it is \(\frac1{-2+1}=-1\).

Step 2: invert entry by entry with \(\frac1{s+a}\leftrightarrow e^{-at}\) \[\mathbf\Phi(t)=\begin{bmatrix}e^{-t}&0\\e^{-t}-e^{-2t}&e^{-2t}\end{bmatrix}.\]

Sanity check: \(\mathbf\Phi(0)=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{bmatrix}=\mathbf I\). ✓ (Moving forward by zero time changes nothing.)

Pitfall. \(e^{\mathbf At}\) is not the matrix of \(e^{a_{ij}t}\) entry by entry. Here the (2,1) entry of \(\mathbf A\) is \(1\), but the (2,1) entry of \(\mathbf\Phi\) is \(e^{-t}-e^{-2t}\), not \(e^{t}\).

3 · ZOH discretisation: \(\mathbf\Phi(T)\) and \(\mathbf\Theta(T)\)

The setup. A zero-order hold (ZOH) keeps \(u(t)=u(k)\) constant for \(kT\le t<(k+1)T\). Integrate the continuous solution across one period with that constant input (Lect 1):

\[\mathbf x(k+1)=\underbrace{\mathbf\Phi(T)}_{\text{free motion}}\mathbf x(k)+\underbrace{\int_0^T\mathbf\Phi(\eta)\,d\eta\;\mathbf B}_{\mathbf\Theta(T)}\,u(k).\]

Why an integral? The input acts during the whole period, and an input applied at time \(\eta\) before the next sample has been propagated by \(\mathbf\Phi(\eta)\). Adding up all of those contributions gives the integral.

Step 1: \(\mathbf\Phi(T)\) by substituting \(T=0.5\) into \(\mathbf\Phi(t)=\begin{bmatrix}e^{-t}&0\\e^{-t}-e^{-2t}&e^{-2t}\end{bmatrix}\) \[\mathbf\Phi=\begin{bmatrix}e^{-0.5}&0\\e^{-0.5}-e^{-1}&e^{-1}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0.6065&0\\0.2387&0.3679\end{bmatrix}.\]
Step 2: \(\mathbf\Theta\). Only column 1 matters, because \(\mathbf B=[1\ 0]^{\mathsf T}\)

\(\mathbf\Phi(\eta)\mathbf B\) picks column 1 of \(\mathbf\Phi(\eta)\): \(\begin{bmatrix}e^{-\eta}\\e^{-\eta}-e^{-2\eta}\end{bmatrix}\). Integrate each entry using \(\int_0^Te^{-a\eta}d\eta=\frac{1-e^{-aT}}a\):

\[\mathbf\Theta=\begin{bmatrix}1-e^{-T}\\(1-e^{-T})-\tfrac12(1-e^{-2T})\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0.3935\\0.3935-0.3161\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0.3935\\0.0774\end{bmatrix}.\]

Neat closed form for row 2: \(\tfrac12(1-e^{-T})^2\).

\[\mathbf x(k+1)=\begin{bmatrix}0.6065&0\\0.2387&0.3679\end{bmatrix}\mathbf x(k)+\begin{bmatrix}0.3935\\0.0774\end{bmatrix}u(k),\qquad y(k)=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\end{bmatrix}\mathbf x(k).\]
Pitfalls.
  • \(\mathbf\Theta\approx\mathbf BT=[0.5\ \ 0]^{\mathsf T}\) is the Euler approximation, not ZOH, and it wrongly gives \(\Theta_2=0\).
  • \(\mathbf C\) does not change under ZOH: the sensor still reads \(x_2\).
  • Keep 4 significant figures. Stage 6 divides by a small determinant, which magnifies rounding.

4 · Can we steer it? Can we see it? \(\mathbf W_C\) and \(\mathbf W_O\)

Controllability (Lect 2). After two steps from \(\mathbf x(0)=\mathbf 0\), \(\mathbf x(2)=\mathbf\Phi\mathbf\Theta\,u(0)+\mathbf\Theta\,u(1)\). Every state is reachable exactly when the columns \(\mathbf\Theta,\ \mathbf\Phi\mathbf\Theta\) span \(\mathbb R^2\), i.e. when \(\mathbf W_C=[\mathbf\Theta\ \ \mathbf\Phi\mathbf\Theta]\) has rank 2.

Step: build \(\mathbf W_C\) from \(\mathbf\Phi=\begin{bmatrix}0.6065&0\\0.2387&0.3679\end{bmatrix}\), \(\mathbf\Theta=\begin{bmatrix}0.3935\\0.0774\end{bmatrix}\) \[\mathbf\Phi\mathbf\Theta=\begin{bmatrix}0.6065(0.3935)\\0.2387(0.3935)+0.3679(0.0774)\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0.2387\\0.1224\end{bmatrix},\qquad \mathbf W_C=\begin{bmatrix}0.3935&0.2387\\0.0774&0.1224\end{bmatrix}.\] \[\det\mathbf W_C=0.3935(0.1224)-0.2387(0.0774)=0.0297\neq0\ \Rightarrow\ \text{controllable}.\]

Observability (Lect 3). From \(y(0)=\mathbf C\mathbf x(0)\) and \(y(1)=\mathbf C\mathbf\Phi\mathbf x(0)\) we can solve for \(\mathbf x(0)\) exactly when \(\mathbf W_O=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf C\\\mathbf C\mathbf\Phi\end{bmatrix}\) is invertible.

Step: build \(\mathbf W_O\) with \(\mathbf C=[0\ \ 1]\)

\(\mathbf C\mathbf\Phi\) is row 2 of \(\mathbf\Phi\): \([0.2387\ \ 0.3679]\).

\[\mathbf W_O=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\0.2387&0.3679\end{bmatrix},\qquad \det\mathbf W_O=0(0.3679)-1(0.2387)=-0.2387\neq0\ \Rightarrow\ \text{observable}.\]

Physically: \(x_1\) is not measured, but it feeds \(x_2\) (\(\Phi_{21}\neq0\)), so its value shows up in \(y\) one step later.

Why this stage gates the next one. Ackermann's formula in Stage 6 contains \(\mathbf W_C^{-1}\). If \(\det\mathbf W_C=0\), stop: some poles cannot be moved. Observability does not affect \(\mathbf K\). It matters only if the state must be estimated (an observer, Lect 4, which is outside the Quiz 2 scope).

5 · The discrete transfer function \(G(z)=\mathbf C(z\mathbf I-\mathbf\Phi)^{-1}\mathbf\Theta\)

This is the same derivation as \(G(s)\) in Stage 1, with \(s\to z\), \(\mathbf A\to\mathbf\Phi\) and \(\mathbf B\to\mathbf\Theta\). It shows where the open-loop poles sit, which are the poles Stage 6 must move.

Step: adjugate of \(z\mathbf I-\mathbf\Phi=\begin{bmatrix}z-0.6065&0\\-0.2387&z-0.3679\end{bmatrix}\) \[(z\mathbf I-\mathbf\Phi)^{-1}=\frac1{(z-0.6065)(z-0.3679)}\begin{bmatrix}z-0.3679&0\\0.2387&z-0.6065\end{bmatrix}.\]

\(\mathbf C=[0\ 1]\) keeps row 2 of the adjugate, \([0.2387\ \ z-0.6065]\). Multiply it by \(\mathbf\Theta=[0.3935\ \ 0.0774]^{\mathsf T}\):

\[G(z)=\frac{0.2387(0.3935)+(z-0.6065)(0.0774)}{(z-0.6065)(z-0.3679)}=\frac{0.0774\,z+0.0470}{(z-0.6065)(z-0.3679)}.\]
Poles \(z=0.6065=e^{-1\cdot T}\) and \(z=0.3679=e^{-2\cdot T}\): the continuous poles \(s=-1,-2\) mapped by \(z=e^{sT}\). Zero at \(z\approx-0.607\).
DC check: \(G(1)=\frac{0.1244}{0.3935\times0.6321}=0.5=G(0)\) from Stage 1. ✓ A ZOH preserves DC gain.

The characteristic polynomial is the denominator, expanded: \(\det(z\mathbf I-\mathbf\Phi)=z^2-0.9744z+0.2231\). Stage 6 replaces it with the polynomial we want.

6 · Design: the deadbeat gain \(\mathbf K\)

Closed loop. Substitute \(u(k)=-\mathbf K\mathbf x(k)\) into \(\mathbf x(k+1)=\mathbf\Phi\mathbf x(k)+\mathbf\Theta u(k)\):

\[\mathbf x(k+1)=(\mathbf\Phi-\mathbf\Theta\mathbf K)\,\mathbf x(k).\]

The design choice is the closed-loop characteristic polynomial \(\alpha_C(z)=\det(z\mathbf I-\mathbf\Phi+\mathbf\Theta\mathbf K)\). Deadbeat puts both poles at \(z=0\), so \(\alpha_C(z)=z^2\). By Cayley–Hamilton, \((\mathbf\Phi-\mathbf\Theta\mathbf K)^2=\mathbf 0\), and every state is wiped out in \(n=2\) steps (Lect 3).

Ackermann (Lect 3): \(\mathbf K=[0\ \ 1]\,\mathbf W_C^{-1}\,\alpha_C(\mathbf\Phi)\), where \(\alpha_C(\mathbf\Phi)=\mathbf\Phi^2\) for deadbeat.

Step 1: \(\mathbf W_C^{-1}\), but only its last row is needed

From Stage 4, \(\mathbf W_C=\begin{bmatrix}0.3935&0.2387\\0.0774&0.1224\end{bmatrix}\) and \(\det\mathbf W_C=0.02968\). Swap the diagonal, negate the off-diagonal, divide by det:

\[\mathbf W_C^{-1}=\frac1{0.02968}\begin{bmatrix}0.1224&-0.2387\\-0.0774&0.3935\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}4.123&-8.041\\-2.608&13.258\end{bmatrix},\qquad [0\ \ 1]\mathbf W_C^{-1}=[-2.608\ \ 13.258].\]

Why the last row? \([0\ 1]\) times a matrix selects its row 2. Ackermann's formula is built so that the gain lands in the last component of the controllable canonical form.

Step 2: \(\alpha_C(\mathbf\Phi)=\mathbf\Phi^2\) \[\mathbf\Phi^2=\begin{bmatrix}0.6065&0\\0.2387&0.3679\end{bmatrix}^2=\begin{bmatrix}0.3679&0\\0.2387(0.6065)+0.3679(0.2387)&0.1353\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0.3679&0\\0.2325&0.1353\end{bmatrix}.\]
Step 3: multiply \([-2.608\ \ 13.258]\,\mathbf\Phi^2\) \[k_1=-2.608(0.3679)+13.258(0.2325)=-0.9595+3.0830=2.1235,\qquad k_2=13.258(0.1353)=1.7942.\]
\[\mathbf K\approx\begin{bmatrix}2.123&1.794\end{bmatrix}.\]
Cross-check by matching coefficients (no inverse needed)

For a \(2\times2\) matrix \(\mathbf M\), \(\det(z\mathbf I-\mathbf M)=z^2-(\operatorname{tr}\mathbf M)z+\det\mathbf M\). With \(\mathbf M=\mathbf\Phi-\mathbf\Theta\mathbf K\), deadbeat requires trace \(=0\) and det \(=0\). Expanding with the Stage-3 numbers:

\[\operatorname{tr}\mathbf M=0.9744-0.3935k_1-0.0774k_2=0,\qquad \det\mathbf M=0.2231-0.1447k_1+0.0470k_2=0.\]

Solving these two linear equations gives \(k_1=2.123\), \(k_2=1.794\): the same \(\mathbf K\). ✓ (Note the constants \(0.9744\) and \(0.2231\): they are the open-loop polynomial \(z^2-0.9744z+0.2231\) from Stage 5.)

Pitfalls.
  • Using the first row of \(\mathbf W_C^{-1}\).
  • \(\alpha_C\) evaluated at \(\mathbf\Phi\), not at \(\mathbf A\) and not at \(z\). It is a matrix.
  • A non-integer \(\mathbf K\) is normal. Past sitters reported fractional gains in this question (see quiz-02-intel.md).

Check: order and steps

A third-order controllable plant with deadbeat feedback is guaranteed to have \(\mathbf x(k)=\mathbf 0\) from:

Recap: the method to remember

  1. Model. Read \(\mathbf A,\mathbf B,\mathbf C\). Optionally get \(G(s)=\mathbf C(s\mathbf I-\mathbf A)^{-1}\mathbf B\) and note \(G(0)\).
  2. \(\mathbf\Phi(t)\). \(\mathcal L^{-1}\{(s\mathbf I-\mathbf A)^{-1}\}\) entry by entry; check \(\mathbf\Phi(0)=\mathbf I\).
  3. ZOH. \(\mathbf\Phi=\mathbf\Phi(T)\), \(\mathbf\Theta=\int_0^T\mathbf\Phi(\eta)d\eta\,\mathbf B\). Integrate only the columns that \(\mathbf B\) selects.
  4. Gate. \(\det[\mathbf\Theta\ \ \mathbf\Phi\mathbf\Theta]\neq0\) (controllable); \(\det\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf C\\\mathbf C\mathbf\Phi\end{bmatrix}\neq0\) (observable).
  5. \(G(z)\). \(\mathbf C(z\mathbf I-\mathbf\Phi)^{-1}\mathbf\Theta\); poles \(=e^{sT}\); check \(G(1)=G(0)\).
  6. \(\mathbf K\). Choose \(\alpha_C(z)\) (deadbeat: \(z^n\)); \(\mathbf K=[0\ \cdots\ 1]\mathbf W_C^{-1}\alpha_C(\mathbf\Phi)\). Cross-check with trace/det.
  7. Verify. \(\mathbf\Phi-\mathbf\Theta\mathbf K\) has the chosen trace and det; iterate from \(\mathbf x(0)\); deadbeat reaches \(\mathbf 0\) in \(n\) steps.
