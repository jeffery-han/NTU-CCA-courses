    Question 1 25 marks

    A signal is given by

    \[x(t)=\begin{cases}0, & t<0\\ 2, & 0\le t<1\\ e^{-(t-1)}, & t\ge 1\end{cases}\]

    and it is sampled with period \(T=0.5\) s.

    (a) 7

    (i) Is \(x(t)\) a continuous-time signal? Is the sampled sequence \(x(kT)\) a digital signal? Give one line of reason for each.

    (ii) Find \(X(z)=\mathcal Z[x(kT)]\) in closed form, starting from the definition of the z-transform.

    (b) 5

    For each of the following, state whether the final value theorem may be applied. Where it may, find \(\lim_{k\to\infty}y(k)\); where it may not, say why and describe what \(y(k)\) does.

    \[Y_1(z)=\frac{0.6\,z^2}{(z-1)(z-0.4)},\qquad Y_2(z)=\frac{z}{(z-1)(z+1)}\]

    (c) 8

    A system is described by

    \[y(k+2)-0.9\,y(k+1)+0.2\,y(k)=x(k),\]

    where \(x(k)\) is the unit step sequence (\(x(k)=1\) for \(k\ge0\), \(x(k)=0\) for \(k<0\)) and \(y(k)=0\) for \(k<0\). Find \(y(0)\) and \(y(1)\), then use the z-transform to find \(y(k)\) in closed form. Check your answer at \(k=2\).

    (d) 5

    A plant \(G(s)=\dfrac{1}{s(s+1)}\) is driven through a zero-order hold, and its output is sampled with \(T=\ln 2\) s. Find the pulse transfer function \(G_{ZAS}(z)\). Give its coefficients to four significant figures.

    Question 2 25 marks

    Figure 1 shows a unity-feedback digital control system with sampling period \(T=0.1\) s. The plant together with its zero-order hold has the pulse transfer function

    \[G_{ZAS}(z)=\frac{0.4\,(z+0.5)}{(z-1)(z-0.6)}.\]

    (a) 8

    Let \(C(z)=K\). Use the Jury test to find the range of \(K\) for which the closed loop is stable. For \(K=1\), find the steady-state error for a unit-step input and for a unit-ramp input \(r(t)=t\).

    (b) 10

    (i) Design the controller \(C(z)\) that gives the closed-loop transfer function \(G_{cl}(z)=z^{-1}\). For a unit-step reference, find \(U(z)\) and the first four values \(u(0),\dots,u(3)\). Explain what this implies for the plant output between sampling instants.

    (ii) Design a ripple-free deadbeat controller for a unit-step reference. Give \(G_{cl}(z)\), \(C(z)\), the sequence \(u(k)\) and the sequence \(y(k)\). After how many sampling periods does the output settle?

    (c) 7

    An analog lead compensator \(D(s)=\dfrac{5(s+1)}{s+5}\) is to be implemented with \(T=0.2\) s.

    (i) Find its digital equivalent \(D(z)\) by pole-zero matching, with the DC gain matched.

    (ii) Realise \(D(z)\) by standard programming: give the difference equations and state how many delay elements are needed.

    Question 3 25 marks

    A continuous-time plant is described by

    \[\ddot y(t)+4\,\dot y(t)+3\,y(t)=u(t).\]

    The input \(u\) is applied through a zero-order hold, and the output is sampled with \(T=\ln 2\) s.

    (a) 9

    With the state variables \(x_1=y\) and \(x_2=\dot y\), write the continuous-time state-space model. Then derive the discrete-time model

    \[\mathbf x(k+1)=\mathbf\Phi\,\mathbf x(k)+\mathbf\Theta\,u(k),\qquad y(k)=\mathbf C\,\mathbf x(k),\]

    giving \(\mathbf\Phi\) and \(\mathbf\Theta\) exactly (as fractions).

    (b) 6

    Find the pulse transfer function \(Y(z)/U(z)\) from your discrete-time model. State the discrete-time poles and check the DC gain against the continuous plant.

    (c) 5

    Is the discrete-time system completely controllable? Is it completely observable from \(y\)? Justify each answer with the relevant matrix.

    (d) 5

    The initial state is \(\mathbf x(0)=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\end{bmatrix}^T\). Find the control sequence \(u(0),u(1)\) that brings the state to \(\mathbf x(2)=\mathbf 0\).

    Question 4 25 marks

    A discrete-time plant is

    \[\mathbf x(k+1)=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\0&0.5\end{bmatrix}\mathbf x(k)+\begin{bmatrix}0\\1\end{bmatrix}u(k),\qquad y(k)=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\end{bmatrix}\mathbf x(k).\]

    (a) 7

    Use Ackermann's formula to find the state-feedback gain \(\mathbf K\), with \(u(k)=-\mathbf K\mathbf x(k)\), that places the closed-loop poles at \(z=0.5\pm j0.3\). Verify your answer.

    (b) 7

    (i) Design a prediction observer whose estimation error is deadbeat. Give the observer gain \(\mathbf L_O\) and the observer equation, and show that the error is zero after two samples for any initial error.

    (ii) Design a current observer, also with deadbeat estimation error. Give its gain \(\mathbf L_C\).

    (c) 5

    (i) The controller of (a) is driven by the estimates of the prediction observer of (b)(i). Write the characteristic polynomial of the complete closed-loop system and name the principle you used.

    (ii) With the true states fed back, the control law is changed to \(u(k)=-\mathbf K\mathbf x(k)+k_r\,r(k)\). Find \(k_r\) so that the output follows a constant reference with zero steady-state error.

    (d) 6

    A scalar plant \(x(k+1)=x(k)+2u(k)\) has \(x(0)=2\).

    (i) Find the optimal control law that minimises \(J=\sum_{k=0}^{1}\left[x^2(k)+u^2(k)\right]\), and the minimum cost.

    (ii) Repeat for the infinite-horizon cost \(J=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left[x^2(k)+u^2(k)\right]\): find the steady-state Riccati solution, the optimal gain, the closed-loop pole, \(u^*(0)\), \(x(1)\) and the minimum cost.
