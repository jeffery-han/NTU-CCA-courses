Q1 · Signals, z-transform, difference equations

Z1. Signals, the z-transform and the final value theorem Refreshevery recalled Q1

Signals. A continuous-time signal is defined for every \(t\), jumps allowed. Sampling it gives a discrete-time signal. If its amplitude is still continuous it is a sampled-data signal; only if the amplitude is also quantised is it digital. Past Q1s open with exactly this classification.

\[X(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x(kT)\,z^{-k},\qquad \lim_{k\to\infty}x(k)=\lim_{z\to1}\,(1-z^{-1})X(z)\] valid only when every pole of \((1-z^{-1})X(z)\) is strictly inside the unit circle.

Example. \(x(kT)=3,1,1,1,\dots\) (a 3 at \(k=0\), then a step of 1): \(X(z)=3+\sum_{k\ge1}z^{-k}=3+\dfrac{1}{z-1}\). Its final value: \((1-z^{-1})X(z)=\dfrac{z-1}{z}\cdot3+\dfrac1z\to 1\) as \(z\to1\). Correct: the sequence settles at 1.

Pitfalls. At a jump, the sample takes the value of the branch that holds at that instant (\(t\ge1\) means the new branch at \(t=1\)). A pole at \(z=-1\) or a double pole at \(z=1\) forbids the FVT: say so, do not compute a number.

Shift theorem: \(\mathcal Z[y(k+1)]=zY(z)-zy(0)\), \(\mathcal Z[y(k+2)]=z^2Y(z)-z^2y(0)-zy(1)\). So you need \(y(0)\) and \(y(1)\). If the question gives \(y(k)=0\) for \(k<0\) instead, compute them by putting \(k=-2\) and \(k=-1\) into the equation (Prof. Wen: do not assume initial values the question does not give).

Q2 · Stability, errors, direct design, implementation

J1. Jury range of K and steady-state errors Refreshrecalled 2019–2023

Closed-loop CE \(P(z)=1+C(z)G(z)\) cleared of fractions, written \(a_0z^n+\dots+a_n\) with \(a_0>0\). For \(n=2\) the Jury test is three inequalities:

\[|a_2|<a_0,\qquad P(1)>0,\qquad P(-1)>0\ (n \text{ even}).\] Type = number of poles of \(CG\) at \(z=1\). \(K_p=\lim_{z\to1}CG\), \(K_v=\lim_{z\to1}\dfrac{(z-1)CG}{Tz}\); step error \(1/(1+K_p)\), ramp error \(1/K_v\).

For \(n=3\) you also need the \(b\)-row: \(|b_2|>|b_0|\) with \(b_0=a_3a_1-a_0a_2\)… you lost this in Quiz 3 Mock D, so practise it from the Quiz 3 primer.

Example. \(CG=\dfrac{K}{z-0.5}\): \(P=z-0.5+K\), root \(0.5-K\), stable for \(-0.5<K<1.5\). Type 0: \(K_p=2K\).

Pitfalls. The range is strict (marginal stability is not stable). Do not forget the \(1/T\) in \(K_v\).

D1. Direct design: deadbeat and ripple-free Exam focus · open mistakerecalled in 5 of 5 papers 2019–2023

You choose the closed loop \(G_{cl}(z)\) you want, then the controller follows from \(G_{cl}=\dfrac{CG}{1+CG}\):

\[C(z)=\frac{G_{cl}(z)}{G(z)\,[1-G_{cl}(z)]},\qquad U(z)=\frac{G_{cl}(z)}{G(z)}R(z),\qquad Y(z)=G_{cl}(z)R(z).\]
Start here: why a plain deadbeat ripples, and how to fix it

Picture. \(G_{cl}=z^{-1}\) means "output equals the reference one sample later". At the sampling instants that is perfect. But \(C\) must contain \(1/G\), so it cancels every plant zero. A cancelled zero at \(z=-b\) becomes a pole of \(U(z)\): the control keeps alternating as \((-b)^k\). The plant integrates those alternating pushes, so between samples \(y(t)\) wobbles: intersample ripple.

Fix. Do not cancel the zero: put it into \(G_{cl}\). Then \(U=G_{cl}R/G\) has no \((-b)^k\) mode and becomes constant after a few samples.

Condition on \(G_{cl}\)Why
relative degree \(\ge\) that of \(G\)\(C\) must be causal
contains the plant zeros you must not cancel (outside the circle, or all of them for ripple-free)otherwise \(C\) has an unstable or ringing pole
\(1-G_{cl}\) contains the plant poles on or outside the circleotherwise \(C\) cancels an unstable pole
\(G_{cl}(1)=1\)zero steady-state error for a step

Recipe for a ripple-free deadbeat (step input), plant \(G=\dfrac{c(z+b)}{(z-1)(z-p)}\): take \(G_{cl}=K\,z^{-1}(1+bz^{-1})=\dfrac{K(z+b)}{z^2}\) and \(K=\dfrac1{1+b}\) from \(G_{cl}(1)=1\). Settling takes one sample more than the plain deadbeat.

Example. \(G=\dfrac{0.5(z+0.2)}{(z-1)(z-0.5)}\). Ripple-free: \(G_{cl}=\dfrac{(z+0.2)}{1.2z^2}\). \(U=\dfrac{G_{cl}}{G}\cdot\dfrac{z}{z-1}=\dfrac{(z-0.5)}{0.6z}=\tfrac53(1-0.5z^{-1})\): \(u=1.667,-0.833,0,0,\dots\) settles, so no ripple. \(y=0,\tfrac1{1.2},1,1,\dots\)

Pitfall from your Quiz 3 mocks. This question was left blank twice. In the final it is worth about 10 marks: write \(G_{cl}\), the four conditions, \(C\), then \(u(k)\) and \(y(k)\) as sequences.

S1. From a continuous model to \(\Phi\) and \(\Theta\) Refresh7 of 7 recalled papers

\[\mathbf x(k+1)=\mathbf\Phi\mathbf x(k)+\mathbf\Theta u(k),\qquad \mathbf\Phi=e^{\mathbf AT}=\mathcal L^{-1}\!\left[(s\mathbf I-\mathbf A)^{-1}\right]_{t=T},\qquad \mathbf\Theta=\int_0^T e^{\mathbf A\tau}\,d\tau\,\mathbf B,\] and \(\mathbf C,\mathbf D\) are unchanged.

S3. Controllability, observability, Ackermann, deadbeat control RefreshAckermann K in 7 of 7

\[\mathbf W_C=[\mathbf B\ \ \mathbf{AB}\ \cdots\ \mathbf A^{n-1}\mathbf B],\quad \mathbf W_O=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf C\\ \mathbf{CA}\\ \vdots\\ \mathbf{CA}^{n-1}\end{bmatrix},\quad \mathbf K=[0\ \cdots\ 0\ 1]\,\mathbf W_C^{-1}\,\alpha_c(\mathbf A),\quad u=-\mathbf K\mathbf x.\]

Deadbeat control: all poles at 0, \(\alpha_c=z^n\), the state reaches zero in at most \(n\) steps. Always verify: \(\det(z\mathbf I-\mathbf A+\mathbf{BK})=\alpha_c(z)\).

Example. Lee's Example 3.12 (double integrator) gives the deadbeat gain \(\mathbf K=\left[\tfrac1{T^2}\ \ \tfrac{3}{2T}\right]\).

Q4 · Observers and optimal control

O1. Prediction observer and its gain Exam focusobserver gain in 4 of 7 recalled papers

Why. State feedback \(u=-\mathbf K\mathbf x\) needs every state, but usually only \(y\) is measured. An observer runs a copy of the model and corrects it with the output error.

\[\hat{\mathbf x}(k+1)=\mathbf A\hat{\mathbf x}(k)+\mathbf Bu(k)+\mathbf L_O\,[y(k)-\mathbf C\hat{\mathbf x}(k)],\qquad \mathbf e(k+1)=(\mathbf A-\mathbf L_O\mathbf C)\,\mathbf e(k),\ \ \mathbf e=\mathbf x-\hat{\mathbf x}.\] \[\mathbf L_O=\alpha_O(\mathbf A)\,\mathbf W_O^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}0\\ \vdots\\ 1\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{(Ackermann, dual of the controller formula)}.\]

Choose the observer poles (roots of \(\alpha_O\)) 2–4 times faster than the controller poles, or all at 0 for a deadbeat error: then \((\mathbf A-\mathbf L_O\mathbf C)^n=\mathbf 0\) and the estimate is exact after \(n\) samples. "Prediction" because \(\hat{\mathbf x}(k+1)\) uses measurements only up to \(k\). The observer needs \((\mathbf A,\mathbf C)\) observable.

Example (Lee Ex 4.3). Double integrator, \(\mathbf C=[1\ 0]\), deadbeat error: \(\mathbf L_O=\left[2\ \ \tfrac1T\right]^T\). With \(T=0.5\): \(\mathbf A-\mathbf L_O\mathbf C=\begin{bmatrix}-1&0.5\\-2&1\end{bmatrix}\), whose square is \(\mathbf 0\).

Pitfall. The observer equation has two inputs, \(u(k)\) and \(y(k)\): write it as \(\hat{\mathbf x}(k+1)=(\mathbf A-\mathbf L_O\mathbf C)\hat{\mathbf x}(k)+\mathbf Bu(k)+\mathbf L_Oy(k)\).

O2. Reduced-order and current observers Exam focusreduced-order: recalled 2019

Reduced-order. If \(y=x_a\) is measured directly, estimate only the rest, \(x_b\). Partition \(\mathbf A=\begin{bmatrix}A_{aa}&A_{ab}\\A_{ba}&A_{bb}\end{bmatrix}\), \(\mathbf B=\begin{bmatrix}B_a\\B_b\end{bmatrix}\):

\[e_b(k+1)=(A_{bb}-L_rA_{ab})\,e_b(k),\qquad \hat x_b(k+1)=(A_{bb}-L_rA_{ab})\hat x_b(k)+L_r\,y(k+1)+(A_{ba}-L_rA_{aa})\,y(k)+(B_b-L_rB_a)\,u(k).\]

For a scalar \(x_b\), \(L_r\) comes from matching \(z-A_{bb}+L_rA_{ab}\) to the desired \(z-p\).

Current observer. Predict, then correct with the measurement taken at the same instant: \(\bar{\mathbf x}(k+1)=\mathbf A\hat{\mathbf x}(k)+\mathbf Bu(k)\), \(\hat{\mathbf x}(k+1)=\bar{\mathbf x}(k+1)+\mathbf L_C[y(k+1)-\mathbf C\bar{\mathbf x}(k+1)]\). Its error matrix is \(\mathbf A-\mathbf L_C\mathbf{CA}\) and

\[\mathbf L_C=\alpha_O(\mathbf A)\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{CA}\\ \vdots\\ \mathbf{CA}^n\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}0\\ \vdots\\ 1\end{bmatrix}.\]

Example (Lee Ex 4.4). \(T=0.1\) double integrator, pole at 0.5: \(z-1+0.1L_r=z-0.5\Rightarrow L_r=5\).

O3. Separation principle, reference gain, integral control Exam focus\(k_r\): recalled 2021, 2022

Separation. Feeding back \(\hat{\mathbf x}\) instead of \(\mathbf x\) gives closed-loop poles = controller poles \(\cup\) observer poles: the characteristic polynomial is \(\alpha_c(z)\,\alpha_O(z)\). Design the two separately.

Reference input. \(u(k)=-\mathbf K\mathbf x(k)+k_r\,r(k)\) gives \(\dfrac{Y(z)}{R(z)}=k_r\,\mathbf C(z\mathbf I-\mathbf A+\mathbf{BK})^{-1}\mathbf B\). \(k_r\) does not move the poles; choose it so the DC gain (\(z=1\)) is 1.

Integral control. Add a state that sums the error. Lee defines the error as \(e=y-r\) (opposite sign to Wen's \(e=r-y\)):

\[x_I(k+1)=x_I(k)+\mathbf C\mathbf x(k)-r(k),\quad \begin{bmatrix}\mathbf x\\x_I\end{bmatrix}(k+1)=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf A&\mathbf 0\\ \mathbf C&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf x\\x_I\end{bmatrix}(k)+\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf B\\0\end{bmatrix}u(k)-\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf 0\\1\end{bmatrix}r(k),\quad u=-[\mathbf K\ \ K_I]\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf x\\x_I\end{bmatrix}+\dots\]

Then place the poles of the augmented system as usual.

Example (Lee Ex 4.8). Servo motor, \(\mathbf K=[4.52\ \ 1.12]\): the final value theorem gives \(k_r=4.52=k_1\). Same pattern whenever \(y=x_1\) and the plant has an integrator.

L1. Linear quadratic regulator: finite horizon Exam focusrecalled 2020, 2022

Idea. Instead of choosing pole positions, choose how much you care about state error (\(\mathbf Q\)) versus control effort (\(\mathbf R\)). Bellman's principle: solve backwards from the last step.

\[J=\tfrac12\mathbf x^T(N)\mathbf S_N\mathbf x(N)+\tfrac12\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\left[\mathbf x^T\mathbf Q\mathbf x+\mathbf u^T\mathbf R\mathbf u\right]\] \[\mathbf K(k)=\left[\mathbf B^T\mathbf S(k+1)\mathbf B+\mathbf R\right]^{-1}\mathbf B^T\mathbf S(k+1)\mathbf A,\qquad \mathbf S(k)=(\mathbf A-\mathbf B\mathbf K(k))^T\mathbf S(k+1)(\mathbf A-\mathbf B\mathbf K(k))+\mathbf K^T(k)\mathbf R\mathbf K(k)+\mathbf Q,\] \[u^*(k)=-\mathbf K(k)\mathbf x(k),\qquad J^*=\tfrac12\mathbf x^T(0)\mathbf S(0)\mathbf x(0).\]

L2. Infinite horizon: the algebraic Riccati equation Exam focusrecalled 2017–2023, 5 times

As \(N\to\infty\), \(\mathbf S(k)\) settles to a constant \(\hat{\mathbf S}\), and the gain becomes constant:

\[\hat{\mathbf S}=\mathbf A^T\hat{\mathbf S}\mathbf A-\mathbf A^T\hat{\mathbf S}\mathbf B\left(\mathbf B^T\hat{\mathbf S}\mathbf B+\mathbf R\right)^{-1}\mathbf B^T\hat{\mathbf S}\mathbf A+\mathbf Q,\qquad \mathbf K=\left(\mathbf B^T\hat{\mathbf S}\mathbf B+\mathbf R\right)^{-1}\mathbf B^T\hat{\mathbf S}\mathbf A.\]

For a scalar plant this is a quadratic in \(S\): take the positive root (the other gives an unstable loop). Stability needs \((\mathbf A,\mathbf B)\) controllable and \((\mathbf A,\mathbf C)\) observable with \(\mathbf Q=\mathbf C^T\mathbf C\). Past papers then ask for \(u^*(k)\), the closed-loop pole and \(\mathbf x(k)\) for a few steps.
