1Deadbeat design (10 marks, blank twice)

Goal: the output reaches the step exactly after a few samples. You pick the closed loop Gcl, then solve for the controller C that produces it.

Plant G(z), step R = 1/(1 − z⁻¹)

Gcl = C·G / (1 + C·G)
 ⇒  C = Gcl / [ G·(1 − Gcl) ]

Y = Gcl·R        U = (Gcl/G)·R

Choosing Gcl: the four conditions

  1. Delay: Gcl needs at least the plant's relative degree d. Start with z⁻ᵈ.
  2. Never cancel a bad zero (on or outside the unit circle). Gcl must keep it.
  3. Unstable or marginal poles such as z = 1: 1 − Gcl must contain them.
  4. Gcl(1) = 1, which gives zero steady-state error.
Plain:        Gcl = z⁻ᵈ
Ripple-free:  Gcl = K·z⁻ᵈ·(1 + b·z⁻¹)
              (keep EVERY plant zero)
              Gcl(1)=1  ⇒  K = 1/(1 + b)

Why plain ripples: C cancels the plant zero at −b. If that zero is near −1, u(k) alternates in sign and decays slowly, so y wobbles between samples even though y(k) = 1 at every sample.

Why ripple-free works: keeping the zero turns U into a finite polynomial, so u settles to its final value (0 for a type-1 plant) in a few steps.

6One-liners for MCQ and True/False

  • Why the w-plane: G(ejωT) is not rational in ω, but G(w) is, so Bode asymptote rules apply. It maps the inside of the unit circle onto the left half-plane (True). It does not stabilise anything and does not remove warping.
  • Lead adds phase and widens bandwidth (faster). Lag raises low-frequency gain (smaller steady-state error) and is slower. "Lag to widen bandwidth" is False.
  • Relative degree: write everything in powers of z first. A factor z⁻³ adds 3.
  • 1-DOF: S + T = 1, so tracking and disturbance rejection can't be shaped separately (True). 2-DOF adds a separate block on r, so Rc, Sc place the poles and Tc, γ shape the response to r.
  • A bad (unstable) plant zero can never be cancelled or moved by feedback, in any structure (False if a question claims otherwise).

Drill 4 · True/False, each with a reason
  1. A controller with 2 poles and 1 zero needs 3 delays in standard programming.
  2. If G has one pole at z = 1, the steady-state error to a unit step is 0.
  3. The plain deadbeat design gives y(k) = 1 for k ≥ d, so it has no ripple.
Answer 4
  1. False. Standard programming needs n = 2 delays; 3 is the direct-programming count.
  2. True. A type-1 loop has Kp = ∞, so ess = 1/(1+Kp) = 0.
  3. False. Samples hitting 1 says nothing about the output between samples. If C cancels a plant zero, u keeps oscillating, so y ripples between samples.
