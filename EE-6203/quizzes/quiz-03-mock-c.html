Section A · Multiple choice 12 marks

A1. The closed-loop characteristic polynomial \(P(z)=a_0z^3+a_1z^2+a_2z+a_3\) (\(a_0\gt0\)) is tested with the Jury criterion. Which condition must hold at \(z=-1\)?

Answer: B

The rule is \((-1)^nP(-1)\gt0\) pp. 164–166. For odd \(n=3\) this is \(P(-1)\lt0\). Reason: with all roots inside the circle, each factor \((-1-p_i)\) is negative for a real root (and a conjugate pair gives a positive product), so the sign is \((-1)^n\). (D) is wrong in this course: stable means strictly inside the unit circle.

A2. An analog controller \(D(s)\) is discretised. Which statement is correct?

Answer: D

\(s=\frac2T\frac{z-1}{z+1}\) sends \(\mathrm{Re}\,s\lt0\) to \(|z|\lt1\), and \(z=1\) to \(s=0\), so stability and DC gain carry over with no extra factor pp. 7–8. (A) describes pole-zero matching, not bilinear. Bilinear poles differ (frequency warping). (B) is wrong: matching the poles and zeros changes the gain, so \(\alpha\) is fixed by the DC gain pp. 14–15. (C) is wrong: the substitution creates the \((z+1)\) factors by itself; adding them by hand is the pole-zero matching rule.

A3. In direct design, \(C(z)=\dfrac{G_{cl}(z)}{G(z)\,[1-G_{cl}(z)]}\). The plant \(G(z)\) has a pole at \(z=1.2\). To keep the loop internally stable you must make sure that

Answer: A

Condition (iii) pp. 51–56: unstable plant poles must be zeros of \(1-G_{cl}\). Then the factor \((z-1.2)\) cancels inside the formula and \(C\) has no zero there. (C) is exactly the cancellation that hides an unstable mode. (B) is the rule for unstable plant zeros, condition (ii).

A4. A 4th-order controller has all four poles clustered near \(z=0.9\) and must run on a processor with short word length. Which implementation is most appropriate?

Answer: C

A pole's error is \(|\Delta p_i|=\left|\sum_j\frac{p_i^{\,n-j}}{\prod_{k\ne i}(p_i-p_k)}\Delta a_j\right|\) pp. 116–119. Clustered poles make the product of differences tiny, so small coefficient errors move the poles a lot. Breaking \(C\) into low-order sections keeps each section's polynomial short pp. 120–125. Direct and standard programming both use the full high-order coefficients. Standard programming saves memory, not accuracy.

Section B · Structured questions 28 marks

B1. (10 marks) A unity-feedback loop has a digital gain \(K\), a ZOH, and the plant \(G_p(s)=\dfrac{1}{s(s+2)}\). The sampling period is \(T=0.2\) s.

(a) Show that the open-loop pulse transfer function is \(G(z)=\dfrac{K(b_1z+b_0)}{(z-1)(z-e^{-0.4})}\) and find \(b_1\), \(b_0\). [3]

(b) Use the Jury test to find the range of \(K\) for closed-loop stability. [5]

(c) With \(K=4\), find \(K_v\) and the steady-state error to a unit ramp. [2]

Worked solution

(a) The ZOH gives \(G(z)=(1-z^{-1})\,\mathcal Z\!\left\{\dfrac{K}{s^2(s+2)}\right\}\). Write \(\dfrac{K}{s^2(s+2)}=\dfrac K2\cdot\dfrac{2}{s^2(s+2)}\) to use the table row with \(a=2\), \(aT=0.4\), \(e^{-0.4}=0.6703\):

\[\mathcal Z\!\left\{\frac{2}{s^2(s+2)}\right\}=\frac{z\,[0.07032z+0.06155]}{2(z-1)^2(z-0.6703)}.\]

Multiply by \(\frac{z-1}{z}\cdot\frac K2\): \(G(z)=\dfrac{K(0.07032z+0.06155)}{4(z-1)(z-0.6703)}=\dfrac{K(0.01758z+0.01539)}{(z-1)(z-0.6703)}\). Same method as Ex 4.8 pp. 174–175.

(b) \(P(z)=(z-1)(z-0.6703)+K(0.01758z+0.01539)=z^2+(0.01758K-1.6703)z+(0.6703+0.01539K)\). For \(n=2\) the three conditions are enough pp. 164–166:

  • \(|a_2|\lt a_0\): \(-1\lt0.6703+0.01539K\lt1\) → \(-108.6\lt K\lt21.42\).
  • \(P(1)=0.03297K\gt0\) → \(K\gt0\).
  • \(n\) even, \(P(-1)\gt0\): \(1+1.6703+0.6703-0.00219K=3.3406-0.00219K\gt0\) → \(K\lt1524\).

Intersection: \(0\lt K\lt21.42\). At \(K=21.42\) the poles are a complex pair on \(|z|=1\).

(c) \(K_v=\dfrac1T\lim_{z\to1}(1-z^{-1})G(z)=\dfrac{1}{0.2}\cdot\dfrac{K(0.03297)}{0.3297}=\dfrac K2\) pp. 186–187. At \(K=4\) (inside the stable range ✓): \(K_v=2\), \(e_{ss}=1/K_v=0.5\). Cross-check: the continuous loop \(K/(s(s+2))\) also has \(K_v=K/2\); a ZOH does not change \(K_v\).

Common slips: forgetting the \(\frac K2\) when using the \(\frac{a}{s^2(s+a)}\) row; using \(\frac{a}{s(s+a)}\) (no ZOH integrator); dropping \(1/T\) in \(K_v\) (gives 0.4 instead of 2).

B2. (8 marks) The continuous system \(G(s)=\dfrac{s+2}{(s+1)(s+4)}\) is to be implemented digitally with \(T=0.2\) s.

(a) Find the strictly proper pole-zero matched \(G(z)=\alpha\,\dfrac{(\cdots)}{(\cdots)}\) with the DC gain matched. Give \(\alpha\). [3]

(b) Find \(G(z)\) by the bilinear transformation and verify its DC gain. [3]

(c) Implement your answer to (b) by standard programming: \(h(k)=x(k)+c_1h(k-1)+c_2h(k-2)\), \(y(k)=d_0h(k)+d_1h(k-1)+d_2h(k-2)\). Give \(c_1\), \(c_2\), \(d_0\). [2]

Worked solution

(a) Map each pole and zero by \(z=e^{sT}\) pp. 14–17: zero \(e^{-0.4}=0.6703\); poles \(e^{-0.2}=0.8187\), \(e^{-0.8}=0.4493\). Here \(n-m=1\), so a strictly proper result adds \(n-m-1=0\) factors \((z+1)\):

\[G(z)=\alpha\frac{z-0.6703}{(z-0.8187)(z-0.4493)}.\]

DC gain: \(G(s{=}0)=\frac{2}{4}=0.5\). So \(\alpha\dfrac{0.3297}{(0.1813)(0.5507)}=0.5\), giving \(\alpha=0.1514\).

(b) \(\frac2T=10\), so \(s=10\frac{z-1}{z+1}\) pp. 7–8. Then \(s+2=\frac{12z-8}{z+1}\), \(s+1=\frac{11z-9}{z+1}\), \(s+4=\frac{14z-6}{z+1}\):

\[G(z)=\frac{(12z-8)(z+1)}{(11z-9)(14z-6)}=\frac{2(3z-2)(z+1)}{(11z-9)(7z-3)}=\frac{6z^2+2z-4}{77z^2-96z+27}.\]

DC: \(G(1)=\frac{6+2-4}{77-96+27}=\frac48=0.5\) ✓. (Poles \(0.818\), \(0.429\): close to the matched \(0.819\), \(0.449\); the faster pole is warped more.)

(c) Divide top and bottom by \(77z^2\): \(G=\dfrac{\frac{6}{77}+\frac{2}{77}z^{-1}-\frac{4}{77}z^{-2}}{1-\frac{96}{77}z^{-1}+\frac{27}{77}z^{-2}}\). Standard programming pp. 108–110 flips the denominator signs in the recursion:

\[h(k)=x(k)+1.2468\,h(k-1)-0.3506\,h(k-2),\qquad y(k)=0.07792\,h(k)+0.02597\,h(k-1)-0.05195\,h(k-2).\]

So \(c_1=96/77=1.2468\), \(c_2=-27/77=-0.3506\), \(d_0=6/77=0.0779\), with 2 delays.

Common slips: adding a \((z+1)\) in (a). That is the proper version, \(\alpha=0.0757\). Forgetting to normalise by 77 in (c). Keeping \(-96/77\) in the recursion instead of flipping it to \(+96/77\).

B3. (10 marks) A unity-feedback system has a digital controller \(C(z)\) in front of a ZOH and plant with

\[G_{ZAS}(z)=\frac{0.5(z+0.4)}{(z-1.5)(z-0.2)}.\]

The reference is a unit step.

(a) Can the desired closed loop \(G_{cl}(z)=z^{-1}\) be used? Check every design condition and justify. [3]

(b) Find the lowest-order \(G_{cl}(z)=a_0z^{-1}+a_1z^{-2}\) that satisfies all the conditions. [3]

(c) Find \(C(z)\) and the output \(y(k)\) for \(k=0,1,2,3\). Comment on the response. [4]

Worked solution

(a) The four conditions pp. 51–56:

  1. Realisable: \(G_{ZAS}\) has relative degree \(2-1=1\); \(z^{-1}\) has relative degree 1 ✓.
  2. Zeros of \(G_{ZAS}\) outside the circle must appear in \(G_{cl}\): the only zero is \(-0.4\), inside ✓.
  3. Unstable poles of \(G_{ZAS}\) must be zeros of \(1-G_{cl}\): \(1-z^{-1}=\frac{z-1}{z}\) is zero only at \(z=1\), not at 1.5 ✗.
  4. \(G_{cl}(1)=1\) ✓.

No. \(C\) would contain a zero at 1.5 that cancels the unstable plant pole, which leaves a hidden unstable mode, so the loop is not internally stable.

(b) Two unknowns, two conditions. From (iv), \(G_{cl}(1)=a_0+a_1=1\). From (iii), \(1-G_{cl}(1.5)=0\), i.e. \(1-\frac{a_0}{1.5}-\frac{a_1}{2.25}=0\), or \(1.5a_0+a_1=2.25\). Subtracting gives \(0.5a_0=1.25\), so \(a_0=2.5\), \(a_1=-1.5\). Check: \(1-G_{cl}=\frac{z^2-2.5z+1.5}{z^2}=\frac{(z-1)(z-1.5)}{z^2}\) ✓. Relative degree is still 1 ✓.

(c) \(C=\dfrac{G_{cl}}{G_{ZAS}(1-G_{cl})}=\dfrac{2.5z-1.5}{z^2}\cdot\dfrac{(z-1.5)(z-0.2)}{0.5(z+0.4)}\cdot\dfrac{z^2}{(z-1)(z-1.5)}\), so

\[C(z)=\frac{5(z-0.6)(z-0.2)}{(z-1)(z+0.4)}.\]

The \((z-1.5)\) cancels inside the formula, between the plant and \(1-G_{cl}\), so \(C\) itself has no zero at 1.5. \(Y=G_{cl}R=(2.5z^{-1}-1.5z^{-2})\frac{z}{z-1}\), which gives \(y=0,\ 2.5,\ 1,\ 1\). The output settles in 2 samples (both closed-loop poles at \(z=0\)) but overshoots by 150 %. That is the price of stabilising an unstable plant with a deadbeat-type design.

Common slips: answering "yes" in (a) because \(G_{cl}(1)=1\); putting the 1.5 condition on \(G_{cl}\) instead of \(1-G_{cl}\); writing \(1-G_{cl}(1.5)\) with \(1.5^{-1}\) but forgetting \(1.5^{-2}=1/2.25\).
