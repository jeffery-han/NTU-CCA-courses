Conventions (LPH Lect 1–3). \(\mathbf x(k+1)=\mathbf\Phi\mathbf x(k)+\mathbf\Theta u(k)\), with \(\mathbf\Phi(T)=e^{\mathbf AT}=\mathcal L^{-1}\{(s\mathbf I-\mathbf A)^{-1}\}\big|_{t=T}\) and \(\mathbf\Theta(T)=\int_0^T\mathbf\Phi(\eta)\,d\eta\;\mathbf B\). Feedback \(u=-\mathbf K\mathbf x\). \(\mathbf W_C=[\mathbf\Theta\ \ \mathbf\Phi\mathbf\Theta]\). Ackermann: \(\mathbf K=[0\ \ 1]\,\mathbf W_C^{-1}\,\alpha_C(\mathbf\Phi)\).

Question 1 · 8 marks — two models, one transfer function

(i) Series RLC circuit (3 marks)

A source \(e(t)\) drives \(R=4\ \Omega\), \(L=1\) H and \(C=\tfrac13\) F in series. Output \(y=v_C\). States \(x_1=v_C\), \(x_2=i\).

Step 1: write the two physical laws. Each energy-storing element gives one first-order equation, so a 2-element circuit has 2 states.

\[\text{KVL around the loop: } L\frac{di}{dt}+Ri+v_C=e,\qquad \text{Capacitor: } C\frac{dv_C}{dt}=i.\]

Why these states? \(v_C\) and \(i\) are exactly the quantities whose derivatives appear, so each law becomes "derivative = combination of states and input".

Step 2: solve each for its derivative and substitute the numbers.

\[\dot x_1=\frac1C\,x_2=3x_2,\qquad \dot x_2=-\frac1L x_1-\frac RL x_2+\frac1L e=-x_1-4x_2+e.\]

Step 3: read off the matrices. Row \(k\) of \(\mathbf A\) holds the coefficients of \(\dot x_k\).

\[\mathbf A=\begin{bmatrix}0&3\\-1&-4\end{bmatrix},\quad \mathbf B=\begin{bmatrix}0\\1\end{bmatrix},\quad \mathbf C=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\end{bmatrix},\quad D=0.\]

(ii) Block diagram (3 marks)

\(u\) enters a summing junction (+), minus \(y\). The error drives \(\dfrac{3}{s+4}\), whose output is \(x_2\). \(x_2\) drives \(\dfrac1s\), whose output is \(x_1=y\).

Method: each block's output is a state; turn every block into an ODE driven by its own input signal.

  • Block \(\frac1s\): input \(x_2\), output \(x_1\). \(sX_1=X_2\Rightarrow \dot x_1=x_2\).
  • Block \(\frac3{s+4}\): input \(u-y=u-x_1\), output \(x_2\). \((s+4)X_2=3(U-X_1)\Rightarrow \dot x_2=-3x_1-4x_2+3u\).
\[\mathbf A=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\-3&-4\end{bmatrix},\quad \mathbf B=\begin{bmatrix}0\\3\end{bmatrix},\quad \mathbf C=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\end{bmatrix}.\]

(iii) Transfer functions and comment (2 marks)

New to this? Step-by-step: how a transfer function is derived from A, B, C, D.

For a \(2\times2\) matrix, \((s\mathbf I-\mathbf A)^{-1}=\dfrac{\operatorname{adj}(s\mathbf I-\mathbf A)}{\det(s\mathbf I-\mathbf A)}\), where the adjugate swaps the diagonal and negates the off-diagonal.

Model (i): \(s\mathbf I-\mathbf A=\begin{bmatrix}s&-3\\1&s+4\end{bmatrix}\), \(\det=s^2+4s+3\), and \(\mathbf C\operatorname{adj}(\cdot)\mathbf B=\) (row 1, column 2 of the adjugate) \(=3\).

Model (ii): \(s\mathbf I-\mathbf A=\begin{bmatrix}s&-1\\3&s+4\end{bmatrix}\), \(\det=s^2+4s+3\), and \(\mathbf C\operatorname{adj}(\cdot)\mathbf B=1\cdot3=3\).

\[\frac{Y(s)}{E(s)}=\frac{Y(s)}{U(s)}=\frac{3}{s^2+4s+3}=\frac{3}{(s+1)(s+3)}.\]

Comment: same transfer function, different state vectors. \(x_1\) is the same in both, but in (ii) \(x_2=\dot x_1=3i\), i.e. \(3\times\) the \(x_2\) of (i). The models are related by the similarity transform \(\mathbf P=\operatorname{diag}(1,3)\): a state-space realisation is not unique, the transfer function is (Lect 2 §5).

Sanity check: block algebra on (ii) gives \(\frac{F}{1+F}\) with \(F=\frac{3}{s(s+4)}\), i.e. \(\frac{3}{s^2+4s+3}\). ✓

Pitfalls
  • \(\dot x_1=i\) without the \(1/C=3\) factor.
  • Losing the minus sign on the feedback (writing \(+3x_1\)).
  • Putting the gain 3 in \(\mathbf C\) instead of \(\mathbf B\): the diagram fixes which signal is \(x_2\).

Question 2 · 10 marks — ZOH discretisation, controllability, \(G(z)\)

\[\dot{\mathbf x}=\begin{bmatrix}-1&0\\1&-2\end{bmatrix}\mathbf x+\begin{bmatrix}1\\0\end{bmatrix}u,\qquad y=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\end{bmatrix}\mathbf x,\qquad T=0.5\text{ s (ZOH)}.\]

(i) \(\mathbf\Phi(T)\) and \(\mathbf\Theta(T)\) (5 marks)

Step 1: resolvent. \(s\mathbf I-\mathbf A=\begin{bmatrix}s+1&0\\-1&s+2\end{bmatrix}\), \(\det=(s+1)(s+2)\); adjugate \(\begin{bmatrix}s+2&0\\1&s+1\end{bmatrix}\). So

\[(s\mathbf I-\mathbf A)^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}\dfrac1{s+1}&0\\[2mm]\dfrac1{(s+1)(s+2)}&\dfrac1{s+2}\end{bmatrix},\qquad \frac1{(s+1)(s+2)}=\frac1{s+1}-\frac1{s+2}.\]

Step 2: inverse Laplace entry by entry (table pair \(\frac1{s+a}\leftrightarrow e^{-at}\)):

\[\mathbf\Phi(t)=\begin{bmatrix}e^{-t}&0\\e^{-t}-e^{-2t}&e^{-2t}\end{bmatrix}\ \Rightarrow\ \mathbf\Phi(0.5)=\begin{bmatrix}0.6065&0\\0.2387&0.3679\end{bmatrix}.\]

Check: \(\mathbf\Phi(0)=\mathbf I\). ✓ (\(e^{-0.5}=0.6065\), \(e^{-1}=0.3679\), difference \(0.2387\).)

Step 3: \(\mathbf\Theta\). \(\mathbf\Phi(\eta)\mathbf B\) with \(\mathbf B=[1\ 0]^{\mathsf T}\) is just column 1 of \(\mathbf\Phi\), so only that column is integrated:

\[\mathbf\Theta=\int_0^T\begin{bmatrix}e^{-\eta}\\e^{-\eta}-e^{-2\eta}\end{bmatrix}d\eta=\begin{bmatrix}1-e^{-T}\\(1-e^{-T})-\tfrac12(1-e^{-2T})\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0.3935\\0.3935-0.3161\end{bmatrix}.\]
\[\mathbf x(k+1)=\begin{bmatrix}0.6065&0\\0.2387&0.3679\end{bmatrix}\mathbf x(k)+\begin{bmatrix}0.3935\\0.0774\end{bmatrix}u(k),\qquad y(k)=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\end{bmatrix}\mathbf x(k).\]

Why the integral? The ZOH holds \(u\) constant over each period, so the state picks up \(\int_0^T e^{\mathbf A\eta}d\eta\,\mathbf B\cdot u(k)\). \(\mathbf\Theta\approx\mathbf BT\) is the Euler shortcut, not ZOH.

(ii) Controllability (2 marks)

\[\mathbf\Phi\mathbf\Theta=\begin{bmatrix}0.6065(0.3935)\\0.2387(0.3935)+0.3679(0.0774)\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0.2387\\0.1224\end{bmatrix},\qquad \mathbf W_C=\begin{bmatrix}0.3935&0.2387\\0.0774&0.1224\end{bmatrix}.\]
\(\det\mathbf W_C=0.3935(0.1224)-0.2387(0.0774)=0.0297\neq0\) ⇒ rank 2 ⇒ controllable.

(iii) \(G(z)=\mathbf C(z\mathbf I-\mathbf\Phi)^{-1}\mathbf\Theta\) (3 marks)

Both routes explained step by step: discrete transfer function explainer.

\[(z\mathbf I-\mathbf\Phi)^{-1}=\frac{1}{(z-0.6065)(z-0.3679)}\begin{bmatrix}z-0.3679&0\\0.2387&z-0.6065\end{bmatrix}.\]

\(\mathbf C=[0\ 1]\) keeps only row 2 of the adjugate; multiply it by \(\mathbf\Theta\):

\[G(z)=\frac{0.2387(0.3935)+(z-0.6065)(0.0774)}{(z-0.6065)(z-0.3679)}.\]
\[G(z)=\frac{0.0774\,z+0.0470}{(z-0.6065)(z-0.3679)},\qquad \text{poles } z=0.6065=e^{-0.5},\ z=0.3679=e^{-1};\ \text{zero } z\approx-0.607.\]

The discrete poles are \(e^{sT}\) at the continuous poles \(s=-1,-2\) (the eigenvalues of \(\mathbf A\)). DC-gain check: \(G(1)=0.1244/0.2488=0.5\), and the continuous \(G(s)=\frac{1}{(s+1)(s+2)}\) has \(G(0)=0.5\). A ZOH preserves DC gain. ✓ The table route \(G(z)=(1-z^{-1})\mathcal Z\{G(s)/s\}\) gives the same answer.

Pitfalls
  • Integrating column 2 (that would be \(\mathbf B=[0\ 1]^{\mathsf T}\)).
  • Using \(\mathbf C=[1\ 0]\) out of habit: this plant measures \(x_2\).
  • Quoting \(-1,-2\) as the discrete poles.
  • Forgetting to swap the diagonal of the adjugate.

Question 3 · 7 marks — deadbeat state feedback

\[\mathbf x(k+1)=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\0&0.5\end{bmatrix}\mathbf x(k)+\begin{bmatrix}0.5\\1\end{bmatrix}u(k),\qquad u(k)=-\mathbf K\mathbf x(k).\]

What "deadbeat" means: put every closed-loop pole at \(z=0\), so \(\alpha_C(z)=z^2\). By Cayley–Hamilton the closed-loop matrix then satisfies \((\mathbf\Phi-\mathbf\Theta\mathbf K)^2=\mathbf 0\), so any initial state is driven to zero in \(n=2\) steps.

(i) Gain \(\mathbf K\) by Ackermann (5 marks)

Step 1: controllability matrix (must be invertible, or no \(\mathbf K\) exists).

\[\mathbf\Phi\mathbf\Theta=\begin{bmatrix}0.5+1\\0.5\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac32\\\tfrac12\end{bmatrix},\qquad \mathbf W_C=\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac12&\tfrac32\\1&\tfrac12\end{bmatrix},\qquad \det\mathbf W_C=\tfrac14-\tfrac32=-\tfrac54\neq0.\]

Step 2: inverse (swap diagonal, negate off-diagonal, divide by det):

\[\mathbf W_C^{-1}=-\tfrac45\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac12&-\tfrac32\\-1&\tfrac12\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}-\tfrac25&\tfrac65\\\tfrac45&-\tfrac25\end{bmatrix}.\]

\([0\ \ 1]\,\mathbf W_C^{-1}\) is the last row: \([\tfrac45\ \ -\tfrac25]\).

Step 3: \(\alpha_C(\mathbf\Phi)=\mathbf\Phi^2\).

\[\mathbf\Phi^2=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\0&\tfrac12\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\0&\tfrac12\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1&\tfrac32\\0&\tfrac14\end{bmatrix}.\]

Step 4: multiply.

\[\mathbf K=\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac45&-\tfrac25\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&\tfrac32\\0&\tfrac14\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac45&\ \tfrac65-\tfrac1{10}\end{bmatrix}.\]
\[\mathbf K=\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac45&\tfrac{11}{10}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0.8&1.1\end{bmatrix}.\]
Alternative: match coefficients (same K)

\(\mathbf\Phi-\mathbf\Theta\mathbf K=\begin{bmatrix}1-\tfrac{k_1}2&1-\tfrac{k_2}2\\-k_1&\tfrac12-k_2\end{bmatrix}\). Its characteristic polynomial is \(z^2-(\text{trace})z+\det\). Deadbeat needs trace \(=0\) and det \(=0\):

\[\tfrac32-\tfrac{k_1}2-k_2=0,\qquad \left(1-\tfrac{k_1}2\right)\left(\tfrac12-k_2\right)+k_1\left(1-\tfrac{k_2}2\right)=\tfrac12+\tfrac34k_1-k_2=0.\]

Subtracting: \(1-\tfrac54k_1=0\Rightarrow k_1=\tfrac45\), then \(k_2=\tfrac32-\tfrac25=\tfrac{11}{10}\). ✓

(ii) Response from \(\mathbf x(0)=[1\ \ 1]^{\mathsf T}\) (2 marks)

\[\mathbf\Theta\mathbf K=\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac25&\tfrac{11}{20}\\\tfrac45&\tfrac{11}{10}\end{bmatrix},\qquad \mathbf\Phi-\mathbf\Theta\mathbf K=\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac35&\tfrac9{20}\\-\tfrac45&-\tfrac35\end{bmatrix}\quad(\text{trace }0,\ \det -\tfrac9{25}+\tfrac9{25}=0).\] \[\mathbf x(1)=\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac35+\tfrac9{20}\\-\tfrac45-\tfrac35\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac{21}{20}\\-\tfrac75\end{bmatrix},\qquad \mathbf x(2)=\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac35\cdot\tfrac{21}{20}-\tfrac9{20}\cdot\tfrac75\\-\tfrac45\cdot\tfrac{21}{20}+\tfrac35\cdot\tfrac75\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0.63-0.63\\-0.84+0.84\end{bmatrix}.\]
\(\mathbf x(1)=[1.05\ \ -1.4]^{\mathsf T}\), \(\mathbf x(2)=\mathbf 0\): the state reaches the origin in \(n=2\) steps and stays there (since \(\mathbf x(k+1)=(\mathbf\Phi-\mathbf\Theta\mathbf K)\mathbf x(k)\) and \(\mathbf x(2)=\mathbf 0\)). That is the deadbeat response.
Pitfalls
  • Using the first row of \(\mathbf W_C^{-1}\) instead of the last.
  • Writing the closed loop as \(\mathbf\Phi+\mathbf\Theta\mathbf K\): with \(u=-\mathbf K\mathbf x\) it is \(\mathbf\Phi-\mathbf\Theta\mathbf K\).
  • Expecting \(\mathbf x(1)=\mathbf 0\): a second-order deadbeat loop needs 2 steps.
