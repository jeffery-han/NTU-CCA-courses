    1 · Sensors (Lec 1)

    Lec 1 has no maths, but it is on the quiz and not the final, so expect several T/F items. Most items test one of these distinctions, so learn them as pairs.

    The pairs

    • Internal (proprioceptive: encoder, potentiometer, tachometer, gyro) vs external (exteroceptive: camera, range, touch). GPS counts as both (lecturer, Week 8).
    • Active emits energy (ultrasonic, laser, IR). Passive only receives it (camera, potentiometer, gyro). Lec 1 p. 19
    • Fusion advantages: redundancy (same feature → less uncertainty, more reliability), complementarity (features no single sensor sees), timeliness, cost. Lec 1 pp. 13–15
    • Accuracy $=1-|m-v|/v$ (closeness to the true value). Precision = repeatability ($\text{range}/\sigma$). Lec 1 p. 25
    • Systematic error can be modelled, so calibrate it out. Random error cannot, so filter, average or fuse. Lec 1 p. 26
    • Integrating sensors (encoders, IMU, gyro) drift: small errors accumulate. Correct the drift with an absolute sensor (GPS, magnetometer).

    Time of flight

    $c\,t$ is the round trip, so the object distance is $d=c\,t/2$ Lec 1 p. 36 p. 38. Example: echo after 40 ms at 340 m/s gives $340\times0.04/2=6.8$ m.
    Accuracy example: true 50, measured 48 gives $1-2/50=0.96$.

    True or false? A sensor that gives the same wrong reading every time is precise but not accurate.

    True. Repeating the same value is precision. Being consistently off is a systematic error, so accuracy is poor, and calibration can fix it.

    An ultrasonic echo returns after 12 ms (c = 340 m/s). Distance to the object in m?

    m

    $d=c\,t/2=340\times0.012/2=2.04$ m. If you got 4.08 m, that is the round trip.

    2 · Pinhole camera (Lec 2)

    The lecturer's two key points: know the three coordinate types, and know $p=Am$. You do not need to memorise the entries of $A$. Know that $f$ is the focal length and $(u_0,v_0)$ is the principal point. Lec 2 p. 30

    Three coordinates

    $$\bar m=\begin{bmatrix}x\\y\\z\end{bmatrix}\ \text{(Euclidean, metres)}\ \xrightarrow{\ \div z\ }\ m=\begin{bmatrix}x/z\\y/z\\1\end{bmatrix}\ \text{(normalised)}\ \xrightarrow{\ A\ }\ p=\begin{bmatrix}u\\v\\1\end{bmatrix}\ \text{(pixels)}$$ With $\alpha=0$: $u=f\sigma_x\,(x/z)+u_0$ and $v=f\sigma_y\,(y/z)+v_0$.

    Depth is lost

    Every point on one ray hits the same pixel. So $m=A^{-1}p$ can be recovered, but $\bar m=z\,m$ cannot without extra information (a known size, a second view, another sensor). Lec 2 p. 17 p. 32

    Example

    $f\sigma_x=f\sigma_y=400$, $(u_0,v_0)=(300,200)$, $\bar m=[0.3,\,0.6,\,3]^{\mathsf T}$ gives $m=[0.1,\,0.2,\,1]^{\mathsf T}$, so $p=(400\cdot0.1+300,\ 400\cdot0.2+200)=(340,\,280)$.

    Also: distortion bends straight lines (systematic, so calibrate it). Defocus only blurs. An 8-bit pixel has 256 levels and takes 1 byte.

    Same camera, $\bar m=[-0.4,\,0.2,\,2]^{\mathsf T}$. Pixel $(u,v)$?

    $m=[-0.2,\,0.1,\,1]^{\mathsf T}$, so $u=400(-0.2)+300=220$ and $v=400(0.1)+200=240$. Divide by $z$ before scaling.

    True or false? If a point slides along its projection ray away from the camera, its pixel coordinates change.

    False. $x/z$ and $y/z$ stay the same along a ray, so $m$ and therefore $p$ are unchanged. That is exactly why depth is lost.

    3 · Essential matrix (Lec 3)

    Two views of the same points, with frames related by rotation $R$ and translation $x$: $\bar m_j=R\,\bar m_j^{*}+x$. The lecturer called slide 14, how to get this constraint, the most important item. Practise writing the three lines below.

    Derivation (three moves)

    1. Write $\bar m=z\,m$: $\ z_j m_j=z_j^*R\,m_j^*+x$.
    2. Premultiply by $[x]_\times$, which removes $x$ because $x\times x=0$: $\ z_j[x]_\times m_j=z_j^*[x]_\times R\,m_j^*$.
    3. Premultiply by $m_j^{\mathsf T}$. The left side is 0 because $x\times m_j\perp m_j$. Divide by $z_j^*>0$: $$m_j^{\mathsf T}E\,m_j^*=0,\qquad E=[x]_\times R.$$

    Consequences

    • The constraint is homogeneous, so $E$ (and $x$) are known only up to scale. Lec 3 p. 15
    • 8-point algorithm: $Me=0$. It needs ≥ 8 points, not all coplanar, and $x\neq0$. Project noisy $E$ onto $\mathrm{diag}\{\sigma,\sigma,0\}$. Lec 3 p. 17
    • Four candidate solutions; positive depth keeps one. Know the idea only; the decomposition on p. 18 is not tested.

    True or false? If the camera only rotates between the two views, the 8-point algorithm still recovers $R$.

    False. $x=0$ makes $E=[0]_\times R=0$, so the constraint holds for any points and contains no information. The essential method needs $x\neq0$. Use the homography instead.

    On paper, closed notes: derive $m_j^{\mathsf T}E\,m_j^*=0$ from $\bar m_j=R\bar m_j^*+x$, giving a reason for each step.

    Model answer: the three moves above. Marks go to (i) substituting $z m$, (ii) $[x]_\times$ with the reason $x\times x=0$, (iii) $m_j^{\mathsf T}$ with the reason "perpendicular", (iv) identifying $E=[x]_\times R$.

    4 · Homography (Lec 3)

    Key points: definition and derivation (slide 31) and how $H$ is computed (slide 32). Decomposing $H$ (pp. 33–36) is not tested.

    Derivation

    The points lie on a plane with normal $n^*$ at distance $d^*$: $n^{*\mathsf T}\bar m_j^*=d^*$, so $\dfrac{n^{*\mathsf T}\bar m_j^*}{d^*}=1$. Multiply $x$ by this 1: $$\bar m_j=R\bar m_j^*+x\frac{n^{*\mathsf T}\bar m_j^*}{d^*}=\underbrace{\Big(R+\frac{x}{d^*}n^{*\mathsf T}\Big)}_{H}\bar m_j^*\ \Rightarrow\ m_j=\alpha_j H m_j^*,\quad \alpha_j=\frac{z_j^*}{z_j}.$$

    Computing $H$

    $H$ has 9 entries but is known only up to scale, so divide by $h_{33}$: 8 unknowns $h=[h_1,\dots,h_8]^{\mathsf T}$. Eliminating $\alpha_j$ leaves 2 equations per point (a $2\times8$ block $M_j$ with $M_jh=m_j$). Stack the blocks into $Mh=m$. So at least 4 coplanar points, no three collinear, are needed. Output: $R$, $x/d^*$, $n^*$ (metric if $d^*$ is known).

    E vs H Lec 3 p. 43

    E: ≥ 8 points, not all coplanar, needs $x\neq0$. H: ≥ 4 points, all coplanar, works for pure rotation, and needs extra information to choose between its solutions.

    True or false? Each matched point gives one equation for $H$, so at least 8 points are needed.

    False. Each point gives 2 equations (u and v, once $\alpha_j$ is eliminated), and there are 8 unknowns, so 4 coplanar points with no three collinear are enough.

    True or false? For a camera that only rotates, $H=R$.

    True. $x=0$ removes the $\frac{x}{d^*}n^{*\mathsf T}$ term. This is why the homography still works where the essential matrix fails (Check 3a).

    5 · Variance rules (Lec 4)

    $V(X)=E[(X-EX)^2]=E[X^2]-(EX)^2$. For uncorrelated $X,Y$: $$V(aX+bY)=a^2V(X)+b^2V(Y)$$ Constants come out squared, so signs disappear: $V(X-Y)=V(X)+V(Y)$. Lec 4 p. 15 Independent implies uncorrelated, but not the other way round.

    Example: $V(X)=1$, $V(Y)=2$ gives $V(2X-3Y)=4\cdot1+9\cdot2=22$.

    Uncorrelated, $V(X)=3$, $V(Y)=1$. $V(X-2Y)=$?

    $1^2\cdot3+(-2)^2\cdot1=3+4=7$. Getting 1 means the sign was carried through; getting 5 means the constant was not squared.

    6 · Kalman filter and fusion (Lec 4)

    Scalar update ($A=H=1$)

    $$\hat x\leftarrow\hat x+K(z-\hat x),\qquad K=\frac{P}{P+R},\qquad P\leftarrow(1-K)P.$$ As $R\to\infty$, $K\to0$ (ignore a useless sensor). As $R\to0$, $K\to1$ (trust the measurement). Lec 4 p. 31

    How the gain is found

    Write the error $\tilde x=x-\hat x$, take its variance (cross terms vanish because the noises are uncorrelated), set $\partial P/\partial K=0$. Mock 1 Section B asks you to do this for two sensors, so it is not worked here. Lec 4 p. 40

    Two sensors, constant state

    $$\hat x=\frac{\sigma_2^2 z_1+\sigma_1^2 z_2}{\sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2},\qquad \frac1P=\frac1{\sigma_1^2}+\frac1{\sigma_2^2}.$$ Each reading is weighted by the other sensor's variance, so the noisier sensor gets less weight. $P$ is smaller than every $\sigma_i^2$. Lec 4 pp. 41–42

    Example: $z_1=20$ ($\sigma_1^2=2$), $z_2=26$ ($\sigma_2^2=4$) gives $\hat x=(4\cdot20+2\cdot26)/6=22$ and $P=8/6=4/3$.

    The filter is optimal only for linear models with Gaussian noise. It does not remove bias, and correlated noise breaks the fusion. Lec 4 p. 45

    $z_1=8$ ($\sigma_1^2=3$), $z_2=12$ ($\sigma_2^2=1$). Fused $\hat x$ and $P$?

    $\hat x=(1\cdot8+3\cdot12)/4=44/4=11$ (closer to the better sensor, $z_2$). $1/P=1/3+1=4/3$, so $P=3/4$. Getting 9 means the weights were swapped.

    True or false? Fusing in a second, much noisier sensor can make the fused variance larger than using the good sensor alone.

    False, provided the noises are independent and unbiased. $1/P$ gains a positive term $1/\sigma_2^2$, so $P$ can only shrink. The noisy sensor just gets a small weight.
