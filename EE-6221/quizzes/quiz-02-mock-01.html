    A1 2

    Calibrating a sensor removes its random errors.

    A2 2

    An ultrasonic range sensor receives the echo of its pulse 30 ms after transmission. Take the speed of sound as 340 m/s. Then the object is 5.1 m from the sensor.

    A3 2

    The main benefit of fusing complementary sensors is a reduction in the uncertainty of a feature that each sensor can already perceive by itself.

    A4 2

    A camera has an intrinsic calibration matrix with \(f\sigma_x = f\sigma_y = 500\) pixels, skew \(\alpha = 0\), and principal point \((u_0, v_0) = (320, 240)\). A feature point has camera-frame coordinates \(\bar m = [0.2,\ -0.1,\ 2]^T\) m. Its pixel coordinates are \((u, v) = (370, 215)\).

    A5 2

    If the pixel coordinates \(p\) of a feature point and the intrinsic matrix \(A\) are known, then the point's Euclidean coordinates in the camera frame are \(\bar m = A^{-1}p\).

    A6 2

    Take eight or more matched feature points in two views, not all coplanar. The essential matrix \(E = [x]_\times R\) estimated from them gives the rotation \(R\) and the translation \(x\), including the magnitude of \(x\).

    A7 2

    Every essential matrix \(E = [x]_\times R\) with \(x \neq 0\) has two equal non-zero singular values and one zero singular value.

    A8 2

    The four-point (homography) method cannot be used when all the matched feature points lie on one plane.

    A9 2

    If random variables \(X\) and \(Y\) are uncorrelated, then \(V(X - Y) = V(X) - V(Y)\).

    A10 2

    Consider the scalar Kalman filter with \(A = H = 1\). As the measurement-noise variance \(R \to \infty\), the Kalman gain \(K \to 0\), so the update effectively ignores the new measurement.

    Section B · Short answer 20 marks

    B · Fusing two sensors 20

    Two sensors \(S_1\) and \(S_2\) measure a constant scalar state \(x\), so \(x_{k+1} = x_k\):

    \[ z_1 = x + v_1, \qquad z_2 = x + v_2 , \]

    where \(v_1\) and \(v_2\) are independent, zero-mean Gaussian noises with variances \(\sigma_1^2\) and \(\sigma_2^2\). Let \(\hat x_k\) be the current estimate, with estimation error \(\tilde x_k = x - \hat x_k\), \(E[\tilde x_k] = 0\) and \(P_k = E[\tilde x_k^2]\). Assume \(\tilde x_k\), \(v_1\) and \(v_2\) are mutually uncorrelated.

    1. The two measurements arrive together and are fused by \[ \hat x_{k+1} = \hat x_k + K_1(z_1 - \hat x_k) + K_2(z_2 - \hat x_k). \] Show that \(P_{k+1} = (1 - K_1 - K_2)^2 P_k + K_1^2\sigma_1^2 + K_2^2\sigma_2^2\). (4)
    2. Find the gains \(K_1\) and \(K_2\) that minimise \(P_{k+1}\). Explain in one sentence which sensor receives the larger gain. (6)
    3. Now the measurements arrive one after the other. \(S_1\) reads \(z_1 = 10\) with \(\sigma_1^2 = 1\); use this as the prior, \(\hat x_0 = z_1\), \(P_0 = \sigma_1^2\). Then \(S_2\) reads \(z_2 = 15\) with \(\sigma_2^2 = 4\). Compute the Kalman gain, the fused estimate \(\hat x\), and its error variance. (6)
    4. A third sensor, with noise variance 4 and independent of the other two, is then fused in the same way. Find the new error variance. Give one reason fusion lowers the variance, and one situation in which this benefit would fail. (4)
