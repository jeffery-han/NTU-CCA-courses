    Q1 · Link coordinates 20 marks

    The six-joint arm in Figure 1 stands on the floor. Joint 1 (\(\theta_1\)) turns the column about its vertical axis. Joints 2 and 3 (\(\theta_2\), \(\theta_3\)) are revolute, with axes perpendicular to the page (⊙). Joint 4 is prismatic: the rod slides out of the forearm by \(d_4\). Joint 5 (\(\theta_5\)) turns the wrist about the vertical axis at the end of the rod. Joint 6 (\(\theta_6\)) rolls the gripper about its own axis, which is 5 cm above the forearm centreline. T is the tool tip. Put frame 0 at the base, on the \(\theta_1\) axis, at floor level.

    (a) 11

    Obtain the link-coordinate diagram using the Denavit-Hartenberg (D-H) algorithm. Show the \(x\), \(y\) and \(z\) axes of every frame, frames 0 to 6.

    (b) 9

    Use your diagram to tabulate the kinematic parameters of all six joints. Then write the link-coordinate transformations \(T^1_0\), \(T^2_1\) and \(T^3_2\). Multiplication is not required. Use \[T^k_{k-1}=\begin{bmatrix}C\theta_k&-C\alpha_kS\theta_k&S\alpha_kS\theta_k&a_kC\theta_k\\S\theta_k&C\alpha_kC\theta_k&-S\alpha_kC\theta_k&a_kS\theta_k\\0&S\alpha_k&C\alpha_k&d_k\\0&0&0&1\end{bmatrix}.\]

    Q2 · Robot control 20 marks

    The dynamic equations of a three-joint robot are

    \[\begin{aligned} (5+2C_2)\ddot q_1+(1+C_2)\ddot q_2-S_2\,(2\dot q_1\dot q_2+\dot q_2^2)+3\dot q_1+d_1&=u_1\\ (1+C_2)\ddot q_1+\ddot q_2+S_2\,\dot q_1^2+9.8\,C_{12}+d_2&=u_2\\ 4\ddot q_3+2\dot q_3+39.2+f+d_3&=u_3 \end{aligned}\]

    Here \(f\) is a contact force and \(d_1,d_2,d_3\) are disturbances. The structure has unmodelled resonances at 2 Hz, 3.5 Hz and 5 Hz. The control system must not excite any of them.

    (a) 9

    Let \(f=0\) and \(d_1=d_2=d_3=0\). Design a computed-torque controller whose error response is overdamped with \(\zeta=1.1\) and as fast as the resonances allow. Give \(\alpha\), \(\beta\), the servo law, the gains and the error equation. Then evaluate the torque \(u_1\) at the instant when \(q_2=\pi/3\), \(\dot q_1=1\), \(\dot q_2=-1\), with \(\ddot q_{1d}=0\), \(e_1=0.05\), \(\dot e_1=-0.1\), \(\ddot q_{2d}=0.2\), \(e_2=-0.02\), \(\dot e_2=0.05\).

    (b) 8

    Joint 3 now presses on a surface. A force sensor measures the contact force, \(f=40\,(q_3-0.3)\). Still take \(d_1=d_2=d_3=0\). Design a hybrid position/force controller. Make the motion subspace overdamped with \(\zeta=1.1\) and the force subspace critically damped, both as fast as the resonances allow. Derive the error equations. Find \(q_3\) at steady state when the desired force is a constant 10 N.

    (c) 3

    The controller of (b) is used, but \(d_3\) is an unknown constant of 2 N. Derive the force error equation and the steady-state force error. How would you remove that error?

    Q3 · Mobile platform and arm 20 marks

    The platform in Figure 2 has a local frame \(\{X_R, Y_R\}\) at point \(P\). Its wheels are:

    • Two fixed standard wheels, A and B, of radius 0.05 m, at \((-0.2,\ 0.15)\) m and \((-0.2,\ -0.15)\) m. Both wheel planes are parallel to \(X_R\), and positive spin drives the platform towards \(+X_R\).
    • One steered standard wheel, C, of radius 0.04 m, at \((0.3,\ 0)\) m, with steering angle \(\beta_C(t)\).

    Measure \(\alpha\), \(\beta\) and \(l\) as in the lecture.

    (a) 10

    Give \(\alpha\), \(\beta\) and \(l\) for wheels A and B. Derive the rolling and sliding constraints of the platform in matrix form. The platform moves with \(\dot x_r=0.4\) m/s and \(\dot\theta=0.5\) rad/s, both in the robot frame. Find \(\dot y_r\), the spin rates of A and B, the steering angle \(\beta_C\in(0,\pi)\), and the spin rate of C.

    A four-joint arm is mounted on the platform. Its joint 3 is prismatic and joint 4 rolls the tool. Its forward kinematics are

    \[T^4_0=\begin{bmatrix}C_{124}&S_{124}&0&0.3C_1+0.2C_{12}\\S_{124}&-C_{124}&0&0.3S_1+0.2S_{12}\\0&0&-1&0.45-q_3\\0&0&0&1\end{bmatrix}\]

    where \(C_{124}=\cos(q_1+q_2+q_4)\), and so on. Lengths are in metres.

    (b) 10

    Write the tool-configuration vector \(w(q)\) and the tool-configuration Jacobian \(V(q)\). Then solve the inverse kinematics for every solution that gives \[w=\begin{bmatrix}0.1&0.4&0.2&0&0&-e^{0.25}\end{bmatrix}^T.\]

    Q4 · Homography 20 marks

    Seven feature points lie on one plane, as shown in Figure 3. \(O_1\), \(O_3\) and \(O_5\) are the corners of a triangle. \(O_2\), \(O_4\) and \(O_6\) are the midpoints of its sides. \(O_7\) is its centroid. A camera takes images from three poses:

    • \(F_a\) sees \(O_1, O_2, O_3, O_6, O_7\);
    • \(F_b\) sees all seven points;
    • \(F_c\) sees \(O_3, O_4, O_5, O_6, O_7\).

    \(G\), \(H\) and \(K\) are the Euclidean homographies from \(F_a\) to \(F_b\), from \(F_b\) to \(F_c\), and from \(F_a\) to \(F_c\). \(G_n\), \(H_n\) and \(K_n\) are the same matrices divided by their (3,3) element.

    (a) 10

    How many four-point groups can be used to derive \(G_n\)? Explain your count, choose a group, and derive \(G_n\).

    (b) 4

    How many four-point groups can be used to derive \(H_n\)? Name them.

    (c) 6

    Can \(K_n\) be obtained? Justify your answer. Would your answer change if \(F_c\) saw only \(O_3\), \(O_4\), \(O_5\) and \(O_7\)?

    Q5 · Sensor integration 20 marks

    Four sensors measure a constant state, \(x_{k+1}=x_k\), with \(y_{ik}=x_k+v_{ik}\) for \(i=1,\dots,4\). The noises \(v_{ik}\) are zero-mean, mutually uncorrelated, and uncorrelated with the estimation error \(\tilde x_k=x_k-\hat x_k\). Their variances are \(a^2\), \(2a^2\), \(4a^2\) and \(4a^2\) for \(S_1\) to \(S_4\). Let \(p_k=E[\tilde x_k^2]\).

    (a) 8

    For \(\hat x_{k+1}=\hat x_k+\sum_{i=1}^4L_{ik}\,(y_{ik}-\hat x_k)\), derive \(p_{k+1}\) and design the gains that minimise it. Evaluate the gains and \(p_{k+1}\) when \(p_k=a^2\).

    (b) 6

    Only one group can be used: group A is \(S_1\) alone; group B is \(S_2\), \(S_3\) and \(S_4\), each with its own optimal gain. Which group gives the smaller estimation error? Justify your answer.

    (c) 6

    Group B is now fused with one gain shared by all three sensors. Design that gain, find \(p_{k+1}\), and compare the result with group A.
