    Q1 · Link coordinates 20 marks

    The six-joint arm in Figure 1 is drawn in the pose where all joint variables are zero. Joint 1 (\(\theta_1\)) turns the column about its vertical axis. Joints 2 and 3 (\(\theta_2\), \(\theta_3\)) have axes perpendicular to the page (⊙). The upper arm points straight up, and the forearm points horizontally to the right. Joint 4 (\(\theta_4\)) rolls the forearm about its own axis. Joint 5 (\(\theta_5\)) has an axis perpendicular to the page at the wrist. The tool hangs straight down from the wrist and rolls about its own axis (\(\theta_6\)). T is the tool tip. Put frame 0 at the base, on the \(\theta_1\) axis, at floor level.

    (a) 11

    Obtain the link-coordinate diagram using the Denavit-Hartenberg (D-H) algorithm. Show the \(x\), \(y\) and \(z\) axes of every frame, frames 0 to 6.

    (b) 9

    Tabulate the kinematic parameters, writing each \(\theta_k\) in terms of the joint variable \(q_k\) for the pose drawn. Then write \(T^1_0\), \(T^2_1\) and \(T^3_2\) with every entry simplified to \(C_k\), \(S_k\) or a number. Use \[T^k_{k-1}=\begin{bmatrix}C\theta_k&-C\alpha_kS\theta_k&S\alpha_kS\theta_k&a_kC\theta_k\\S\theta_k&C\alpha_kC\theta_k&-S\alpha_kC\theta_k&a_kS\theta_k\\0&S\alpha_k&C\alpha_k&d_k\\0&0&0&1\end{bmatrix}.\]

    Q2 · Robot control 20 marks

    The dynamic equations of a three-joint robot are

    \[\begin{aligned} (4+C_2)\ddot q_1+(0.5+0.5C_2)\ddot q_2-0.5S_2\,(2\dot q_1\dot q_2+\dot q_2^2)+2\dot q_1+14.7C_1+4.9C_{12}+d_1&=u_1\\ (0.5+0.5C_2)\ddot q_1+0.5\ddot q_2+0.5S_2\,\dot q_1^2+4.9\,C_{12}+d_2&=u_2\\ 6\ddot q_3+3\dot q_3+58.8+f+d_3&=u_3 \end{aligned}\]

    Here \(f\) is a contact force and \(d_1,d_2,d_3\) are disturbances. The structure has unmodelled resonances at 4.5 Hz, 3 Hz and 7 Hz.

    (a) 9

    Let \(f=0\) and \(d_1=d_2=d_3=0\). Design a computed-torque controller with \(\zeta=1.2\), as fast as the resonances allow. Write \(\alpha\), \(\beta\), the full servo law \(v\), the gains and the error equation. Evaluate \(u_2\) at the instant when \(q_1=\pi/3\), \(q_2=0\), \(\dot q_1=1\), \(\dot q_2=0.5\), with \(\ddot q_{1d}=0\), \(e_1=0.02\), \(\dot e_1=0\), \(\ddot q_{2d}=0.4\), \(e_2=0.01\), \(\dot e_2=-0.05\).

    (b) 8

    Joint 3 now presses on a surface, and a sensor measures \(f=60\,(q_3-0.15)\). Take \(d=0\). Design a hybrid position/force controller with the motion subspace as in (a) and the force subspace critically damped, as fast as allowed. Write the force servo law and \(u_3\), and derive both error equations. Evaluate \(u_3\) when \(\dot q_3=0.1\), the sensor reads \(f=12\) N, and the desired force is a constant 18 N. Find \(q_3\) at steady state.

    (c) 3

    With the controller of (b), \(d_3\) is an unknown constant of 3 N. Derive the force error equation, find the steady-state force error, and state how to remove it.

    Q3 · Mobile platform and arm 20 marks

    The platform in Figure 2 has a local frame \(\{X_R, Y_R\}\) at \(P\). It has:

    • two fixed standard wheels, A at \((-0.24,\ 0.07)\) m and B at \((-0.24,\ -0.07)\) m, radius 0.05 m. Both wheel planes are parallel to \(X_R\), and positive spin drives the platform towards \(+X_R\);
    • one steered standard wheel, C at \((0.30,\ 0.10)\) m, radius 0.04 m, with steering angle \(\beta_C(t)\).

    Measure \(\alpha\), \(\beta\) and \(l\) as in the lecture.

    (a) 10

    Give \(l\), \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) for wheels A, B and C. Derive the rolling and sliding constraints of the platform in matrix form, keeping \(\beta_C\) symbolic. The platform moves with \(\dot x_r=0.6\) m/s and \(\dot\theta=-0.5\) rad/s in the robot frame. Find \(\dot y_r\), the spin rates of A and B, and the steering angle \(\beta_C\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)\) and spin rate of C for which \(\dot\varphi_C\gt0\).

    A four-joint arm on the platform has joint 3 prismatic and joint 4 rolling the tool, with

    \[T^4_0=\begin{bmatrix}C_{124}&S_{124}&0&0.35C_1+0.25C_{12}\\S_{124}&-C_{124}&0&0.35S_1+0.25S_{12}\\0&0&-1&0.5-q_3\\0&0&0&1\end{bmatrix}\]

    in metres.

    (b) 10

    Write the tool-configuration vector \(w(q)\) and the tool-configuration Jacobian \(V(q)\). Then find every solution of the inverse kinematics for \[w=\begin{bmatrix}0.3&0.35&0.18&0&0&-e^{0.75}\end{bmatrix}^T.\]

    Q4 · Homography 20 marks

    Seven feature points lie on one plane (Figure 3): the corners of a rectangle \(O_1(0,0)\), \(O_2(4,0)\), \(O_3(4,2)\) and \(O_4(0,2)\); its centre \(O_5(2,1)\); and the midpoints \(O_6(2,0)\) and \(O_7(2,2)\) of its long sides. A camera takes images from three poses:

    • \(F_a\) sees \(O_1, O_2, O_4, O_6, O_7\);
    • \(F_b\) sees all seven points;
    • \(F_c\) sees \(O_3, O_4, O_5, O_6, O_7\).

    \(G\), \(H\) and \(K\) are the Euclidean homographies from \(F_a\) to \(F_b\), from \(F_b\) to \(F_c\), and from \(F_a\) to \(F_c\). \(G_n\), \(H_n\) and \(K_n\) are the same matrices divided by their (3,3) element.

    (a) 10

    How many four-point groups can be used to derive \(G_n\)? List them and explain why every other group fails. Derive the linear system that gives \(G_n\) from one group. Then write the two equations contributed by \(O_7\) if it appears at \((1.5,\ 2.2)\) in the \(F_a\) image and at \((2.1,\ 1.8)\) in the \(F_b\) image.

    (b) 4

    How many four-point groups can be used to derive \(H_n\)? Name them.

    (c) 6

    Can \(K_n\) be obtained? Justify your answer and give the expression if it can. Would your answer change if \(F_b\) did not see \(O_6\)?

    Q5 · Sensor integration 20 marks

    Three sensors measure a constant state, \(x_{k+1}=x_k\), with \(y_{ik}=x_k+v_{ik}\). The noises are zero-mean, mutually uncorrelated and uncorrelated with \(\tilde x_k=x_k-\hat x_k\). Their variances are \(3a^2\) for \(S_1\), \(6a^2\) for \(S_2\) and \(a^2\) for \(S_3\). Let \(p_k=E[\tilde x_k^2]\).

    (a) 8

    For \(\hat x_{k+1}=\hat x_k+\sum_{i=1}^3L_{ik}(y_{ik}-\hat x_k)\), derive \(p_{k+1}\) in terms of the gains, then derive the gains that minimise it. Show every step. Evaluate the gains and \(p_{k+1}\) for \(p_k=2a^2\).

    (b) 6

    Only one group can be used: group A is \(S_3\) alone; group B is \(S_1\) and \(S_2\), each with its own optimal gain. Which group gives the smaller \(p_{k+1}\)? Justify your answer.

    (c) 6

    All three sensors are now fused with one shared gain. Derive that gain and \(p_{k+1}\) for \(p_k=2a^2\). Is this better than using \(S_3\) alone with its optimal gain? Explain why.
