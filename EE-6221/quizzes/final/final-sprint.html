1 · Q1: D-H offset matrices Mistake #6

Rule: \(\cos(q - 90^\circ) = +S_q\), \(\sin(q - 90^\circ) = -C_q\); \(\cos(q + 90^\circ) = -S_q\), \(\sin(q + 90^\circ) = +C_q\). Sanity check at \(q = 90^\circ\)!

Worked check With \(\theta_3 = q_3 - 90^\circ\) and \(\alpha_3 = +90^\circ\) (\(S\alpha=1, C\alpha=0\)): \(C\theta_3 = +S_3\), \(S\theta_3 = -C_3\). So col 1 is \([S_3, -C_3, 0]^T\) and col 3 is \([-C_3, -S_3, 0]^T\). At \(q_3 = 90^\circ\), \(\theta_3 = 0^\circ\) and col 1 \(= [1, 0, 0]^T\)!

2 · Q2: Hz resonances, no \(d\) in \(\beta\), and \(\alpha^{-1} d\) Mistakes #7, #15, #16

Three fast fixes recover every lost mark in Q2. Lec 2 pp. 6–14 (partitioned control, gain limit, disturbances)

Two traps from 2025–26 S1 Q2(b):
1. Never put unknown disturbances \(d_1, d_2\) inside \(\beta'\)! Only known/measured terms (like \(f\)) go in \(\beta\).
2. Never write \(\ddot e + k_v \dot e + k_p e = d/m\) when \(\alpha\) has off-diagonal coupling! Keep \(\alpha\) on the left: \(\alpha(\ddot e + K_v \dot e + K_p e) = d\), then multiply by \(\alpha^{-1}\).

Worked check \(\omega_{\min} = 2\pi(2.5) = 5\pi \approx 15.7080\text{ rad/s}\), \(\omega_n = 2.5\pi \approx 7.8540\text{ rad/s}\), \(k_p = \omega_n^2 \approx 61.6850\), \(k_v = 2(1.2)(2.5\pi) = 6\pi \approx 18.8496\).

5 · Q5: Kalman full 4-step derivation Mistake #19

Lock in the 4-line chain from the error recursion to the information form. HGQ Lec 4 pp. 25–31 (two-sensor Kalman) · pp. 39–44 (n sensors & comparison)

1. \(p_{k+1} = C^2 p_k + \sum_i L_i^2 \sigma_i^2\) (PLUS, never minus!) where \(C = 1 - \sum_j L_j\).
2. \(\frac{\partial p_{k+1}}{\partial L_i} = -2 C p_k + 2 L_i \sigma_i^2 = 0\) (keep \(p_k\)!) \(\implies L_i = \frac{C p_k}{\sigma_i^2}\).
3. Plug back: \(p_{k+1} = C p_k \implies \frac{1}{p_{k+1}} = \frac{1}{p_k} + \sum_i \frac{1}{\sigma_i^2}\), and \(L_i = \frac{p_{k+1}}{\sigma_i^2} = \frac{p_k}{\sigma_i^2 (1 + p_k \sum_j 1/\sigma_j^2)}\).

Check 5.1. True or False: In \(p_{k+1} = (1 - L_1 - L_2)^2 p_k - L_1^2 \sigma_1^2 - L_2^2 \sigma_2^2\), the minus signs come from \(-L_i v_i\) in \(\tilde x_{k+1}\).

Worked check False. \(E[(-L_i v_i)^2] = +L_i^2 \sigma_i^2\).

Check 5.2. Two sensors have variances \(\sigma_1^2 = 2a^2\), \(\sigma_2^2 = 6a^2\), and prior variance \(p_k = 3a^2\). Compute (in units of \(a^2\) or \(1/a^2\)):
Added information \(\sum 1/\sigma_i^2\) (in \(1/a^2\)) = , \(1/p_{k+1}\) (in \(1/a^2\)) = , \(p_{k+1}/a^2 =\) , \(L_1 =\) , \(L_2 =\)

Hint\(\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{2}{3}\). Then \(\frac{1}{p_{k+1}} = \frac{1}{3} + \frac{2}{3}\).
Worked check \(\sum 1/\sigma_i^2 = 1/2 + 1/6 = 2/3\). \(1/p_{k+1} = 1/3 + 2/3 = 1 \implies p_{k+1} = a^2\). Thus \(L_1 = p_{k+1}/\sigma_1^2 = 1/2\) and \(L_2 = 1/6\).

Check 5.3. Group 1 uses \(\{S_1, S_2\}\) above (\(\sigma_1^2=2a^2, \sigma_2^2=6a^2\)). Group 2 uses three sensors with variances \(4a^2, 4a^2, 4a^2\). Which group gives the smaller estimation error \(p_{k+1}\)?

Worked check Group 2 adds \(\frac{3}{4a^2} > \frac{2}{3a^2}\), so \(1/p_{k+1,2} > 1/p_{k+1,1} \implies p_{k+1,2} < p_{k+1,1}\).
