Question 1 — D–H modelling of a cylindrical robot (12 marks)

The robot in Figure 1 has three joints:

  • Joint 1 — revolute: rotates the whole arm about the fixed vertical column; joint angle \(\theta_1\).
  • Joint 2 — prismatic: a carriage slides vertically up and down the column; displacement \(d_2\).
  • Joint 3 — prismatic: the horizontal arm telescopes radially outward; displacement \(d_3\).

A rigid tool of length \(L_t\) is fixed to the end of link 3, pointing along the direction of extension.

  1. (a) Assign a coordinate frame to each link and draw the link-coordinate diagram. Show every \(z_k\) and \(x_k\); state where each origin sits. (4)
  2. (b) Tabulate the four D–H parameters \((\theta_k, d_k, a_k, \alpha_k)\) for all three joints. Mark which parameter is the joint variable in each row. (3)
  3. (c) Write the three link transformation matrices \({}^{0}T_1\), \({}^{1}T_2\), \({}^{2}T_3\). (3)
  4. (d) Form the arm matrix \({}^{0}T_{\text{tool}}\) to the tool tip (include the tool length \(L_t\)). Identify the orientation block \(R\) and the position \(p\), and verify \(R\) is orthonormal. (2)

Question 2 — pick-and-place arm matrix (8 marks)

Figure 2 is a top view of a workstation. The base frame \(\{0\}\) has \(z_0\) vertically up; all coordinates are in centimetres.

  • Part C: a rectangular block, centroid \({}^{0}p_C = [4,\ 8,\ 2]^{\mathsf T}\), lying flat, its long axis along \(x_0\), short axis along \(y_0\); total height \(4\).
  • Part D: a rectangular block, centroid \({}^{0}p_D = [9,\ 3,\ 1]^{\mathsf T}\), long axis along \(x_0\); total height \(2\).

Tool-frame convention (as in your notes): \(R = [\,r_1\ r_2\ r_3\,]\) are the tool normal, sliding, and approach vectors; \(r_3\) points the way the tool advances toward the part, \(r_2\) lies along the gripper's closing direction.

  1. (a) Find the arm-matrix value \({}^{0}T_{\text{pick}}\) needed to pick part C from above by grasping its long sides. (4)
  2. (b) Find \({}^{0}T_{\text{place}}\) needed to set part C on top of part D with the centroids and the long axes aligned. (3)
  3. (c) Verify that the rotation block of your answer satisfies \(R^{\mathsf T}R = I\) and \(\det R = +1\). (1)

Section C — predicted extension (5 marks)

Not seen on any recalled Quiz 1 paper. In scope per the Week 3 lecture remarks and the official practice handout (Problem 1c). Attempt if time remains; a low score here is not a core-topic gap.
  1. C1. Write the tool configuration vector \(w \in \mathbb{R}^6\) for the cylindrical robot of Question 1. State clearly what each part of \(w\) is and why the lower three components take the value they do for this arm. (2)
  2. C2. Solve the inverse kinematics of the Question 1 arm: given a target tool-tip position \((x, y, z)\), find \(\theta_1\), \(d_2\), \(d_3\). Use \(\operatorname{atan2}\) where a quadrant matters, state the feasibility condition, and say whether the solution is unique. (3)
