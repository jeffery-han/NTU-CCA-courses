Question 1 · Genetic algorithms 25 marks

(a) 15 marks

The 8-queens problem is solved with a GA using permutation coding: gene \(i\) is the row of the queen in column \(i\), and a chromosome is a permutation of \(1,\dots,8\). The fitness of a chromosome \(q\) is \[ f(q) = 1-\frac{C(q)}{28}, \] where \(C(q)\) is the number of pairs of queens that attack each other. Two parents are \[ P_1 = 6\ 2\ 1\ 3\ 5\ 8\ 4\ 7, \qquad P_2 = 5\ 1\ 8\ 2\ 7\ 6\ 3\ 4. \]

  1. Explain why this coding removes all row and column conflicts, and compare the size of its search space with that of a coding that lets each gene take any value \(1,\dots,8\) independently. (3)
  2. Calculate \(C\) and \(f\) for \(P_1\) and for \(P_2\). List the attacking pairs. (4)
  3. Apply partially mapped crossover (PMX), copying the segment at positions 3–6 from \(P_1\). Show every mapping step and give the child. (5)
  4. Calculate \(C\) and \(f\) for the child. (2)
  5. Explain why the child is a valid chromosome, and name one standard crossover that would not guarantee this for this coding. (1)

(b) 10 marks

A GA minimises a cost \(g(x)\). The current population of five individuals has costs \[ g = (4,\ 9,\ 1,\ 6,\ 10) \quad\text{for individuals } 1,\dots,5. \] Parents are chosen by roulette-wheel (fitness-proportionate) selection.

  1. Explain why \(g\) itself cannot be used as the fitness in roulette-wheel selection. (2)
  2. Using the fitness \(f = 1/(1+g)\), calculate each individual's selection probability. (4)
  3. How many copies of individual 3 are expected in a mating pool of size 5? (1)
  4. The wheel is spun three times with uniform random numbers \(u = 0.25,\ 0.80,\ 0.95\). Lay the individuals out on the wheel in order 1 to 5 and state which individual each spin selects. (2)
  5. Comment on your result in (iv) in terms of exploration and exploitation. (1)

Classify the new message “free notes now free”. Show the priors, the vocabulary size, every smoothed word probability you need, and both class scores. Explain briefly why smoothing is needed here. (10)

(b) 8 marks

A Gaussian naive Bayes classifier has two continuous features. Its estimated parameters are:

ClassPriorMean \((\mu_1,\mu_2)\)Variance \((\sigma_1^2,\sigma_2^2)\)
\(\omega_1\)0.6(2, 5)(1, 4)

  1. Calculate the class-conditional likelihoods \(p(\mathbf x\mid\omega_1)\) and \(p(\mathbf x\mid\omega_2)\) for \(\mathbf x=(3,4)\). (3)
  2. Classify \(\mathbf x\) with the Bayes decision rule, using log-discriminants. (3)

Question 3 · Fisher LDA and SVM 25 marks

(a) 14 marks

A two-class problem has class means and scatter matrices

\[ \mathbf m_1=\begin{bmatrix}6\\4\end{bmatrix},\quad \mathbf m_2=\begin{bmatrix}2\\2\end{bmatrix},\quad \mathbf S_1=\begin{bmatrix}30&10\\10&20\end{bmatrix},\quad \mathbf S_2=\begin{bmatrix}26&-2\\-2&16\end{bmatrix}. \]
  1. State the Fisher criterion that is maximised, define \(\mathbf S_B\) and \(\mathbf S_W\), and explain in words what the criterion achieves. (3)
  2. Calculate \(\mathbf S_W\). (1)
  3. Calculate the Fisher weight vector \(\mathbf w\). (4)
  4. Calculate the bias \(w_0\) that places the boundary midway between the projected means. (2)
  5. Classify \(\mathbf x=(3.5,\ 3)\). (2)
  6. Calculate the distance of \(\mathbf x\) from the decision boundary and comment on the confidence of the decision. (2)

(b) 11 marks

A soft-margin linear SVM with \(C = 1\) is trained. The training samples with their labels \(d_i\) and the optimal Lagrange multipliers \(\alpha_i\) are:

\(i\)\(\mathbf x_i\)\(d_i\)\(\alpha_i\)
1(2, 3)+10.25
2(4, 1)+10.5
3(1, 2)−10.875
4(3, 0)−10.875
5(2, 1.5)+11
6(5, 4)+10
  1. Verify that the multipliers satisfy the equality constraint of the dual problem. (1)
  2. Calculate the optimal weight vector \(\mathbf w\). (3)
  3. State which samples should be used to calculate the bias \(b\), and why. (2)
  4. Calculate \(b\). (2)
  5. Calculate the width of the margin, and the slack \(\xi_5\) of sample 5. What does \(\xi_5\) tell you? (1)
  6. Classify \(\mathbf x=(3.2,\ 1.2)\), give its distance from the decision boundary, and comment on the confidence. (2)

Question 4 · Clustering 25 marks

Six samples in two dimensions are given. Use the Euclidean distance throughout.

SampleABCDEF
\((x_1, x_2)\)(0, 0)(1, 2)(4, 0)(8, 7)(9, 4)(12, 8)

(a) 10 marks

Perform agglomerative hierarchical clustering with single linkage. Give the initial distance matrix and the updated distance matrix after every merge, draw the dendrogram with heights proportional to the merge distances, and state the result when the dendrogram is cut into two clusters. (10)

(b) 7 marks

For the two-cluster result of (a), calculate the Davies–Bouldin index. Show the centroids, the within-cluster dispersions and the separation, and interpret the value. (7)

(c) 4 marks

For the same two-cluster result, calculate the Dunn index and interpret it. (4)

(d) 4 marks

A second clustering method gives the three clusters \(\{A, B\}\), \(\{C, D, E\}\), \(\{F\}\). Calculate the Rand index between this result and the two-cluster result of (a). (4)
