Q1 · Genetic algorithms 25 marks

(a) Binary decoding 10 marks

A GA minimises \(f(x,y)=x^2+2y^2-3x\) with \(x\in[-4,4]\) and \(y\in[0,6]\). Each variable is encoded with 8 bits, \(x\) first. One chromosome is 10110100 01011010. GA part 1, pp. 20 & 31

1. Decimal value of the \(x\) field 10110100:

HintBit weights from the left are 128, 64, 32, 16, 8, 4, 2, 1. Add the weights where the bit is 1.
Worked line\(128+32+16+4=180\).

2. Decoded \(x\):

Hint\(x=a+D\,\frac{b-a}{2^n-1}\) with \(2^8-1=255\).
Worked line\(x=-4+180\cdot\frac{8}{255}=-4+5.6471=1.6471\). Check that it lies inside \([-4,4]\).

3. Decimal value of the \(y\) field 01011010:

Worked line\(64+16+8+2=90\).

4. Decoded \(y\):

Worked line\(y=0+90\cdot\frac{6}{255}=2.1176\).

5. \(f(x,y)\) for this chromosome:

Worked line\(x^2=2.7128\), \(2y^2=8.9689\), \(-3x=-4.9412\), so \(f=6.7405\). Keep 4 or more decimals in \(x\) and \(y\) until the end.

6. Resolution (smallest representable step) of \(x\):

Worked line\(\frac{8}{255}=0.0314\). The grid is \(-4,\,-3.9686,\,\dots,\,4\).

7. Minimum number of bits for \(x\) to have a resolution of 0.001 or finer:

HintNeed \(\frac{8}{2^n-1}\le 0.001\), that is, \(2^n\ge 8001\).
Worked line\(2^{12}=4096<8001\le 8192=2^{13}\), so \(n=13\) (resolution \(8/8191=0.00098\)).

written 8. State two limitations of this binary representation for a continuous problem. (2 marks)

Model answer(i) Only \(2^n\) grid values can be reached; the true optimum \(x^\*=0.75,\ y^\*=0\) is approximated only to within the resolution, and more precision needs longer chromosomes and a larger search space. (ii) Hamming cliffs: neighbouring values such as 01111111 → 10000000 differ in every bit, so a small move in \(x\) needs many simultaneous flips, while flipping one high bit jumps by half the range. Gray coding or real-valued coding fixes this. One mark each.

(b) Real-coded crossover with a constraint 15 marks

A portfolio of 4 assets is coded as weights \(\mathbf w=(w_1,\dots,w_4)\) with \(w_i\ge 0\) and \(\sum w_i=1\). Maximise \(F(\mathbf w)=\sum_i\mu_i w_i-0.1\sum_i w_i^2\), where \(\boldsymbol\mu=(0.08,\,0.12,\,0.10,\,0.06)\). The parents are \(\mathbf P_1=(0.40,\,0.10,\,0.30,\,0.20)\) and \(\mathbf P_2=(0.10,\,0.50,\,0.20,\,0.20)\). GA part 2, slides 23–28

1. \(F(\mathbf P_1)\):

Worked line\(\boldsymbol\mu\cdot\mathbf P_1=0.032+0.012+0.030+0.012=0.086\); \(\sum w^2=0.16+0.01+0.09+0.04=0.30\); \(F=0.086-0.030=0.0560\).

2. \(F(\mathbf P_2)\):

Worked line\(0.008+0.060+0.020+0.012=0.100\); \(\sum w^2=0.34\); \(F=0.100-0.034=0.0660\).

3. Whole arithmetic crossover with \(\alpha=0.7\), child 1 \(=0.7\mathbf P_1+0.3\mathbf P_2\):

HintApply the same \(\alpha\) to every gene.
Worked line\((0.28+0.03,\ 0.07+0.15,\ 0.21+0.06,\ 0.14+0.06)=(0.31,\,0.22,\,0.27,\,0.20)\), sum = 1.

4. Child 2 \(=0.3\mathbf P_1+0.7\mathbf P_2\):

Worked line\((0.19,\,0.38,\,0.23,\,0.20)\), sum = 1.

5. \(F(\text{child 1})\):

Worked line\(0.0248+0.0264+0.0270+0.0120=0.0902\); \(\sum w^2=0.0961+0.0484+0.0729+0.04=0.2574\); \(F=0.0902-0.02574=0.0645\). This is better than \(\mathbf P_1\) and worse than \(\mathbf P_2\), as you would expect from a point between them.

6. Blend crossover BLX-\(\alpha\) with \(\alpha=0.5\), defined here as \(z_i=\min_i-\alpha d_i+u_i(1+2\alpha)d_i\), \(d_i=|P_{1i}-P_{2i}|\), random numbers \(\mathbf u=(0.1,\,0.9,\,0.5,\,0.3)\). Child \(\mathbf z\):

HintFor each gene: interval \([\min-0.5d,\ \max+0.5d]\), which has width \(2d\). Then \(z=\text{left end}+u\cdot 2d\).
Worked lineGene 1: \(d=0.3\), interval \([-0.05,0.55]\), \(z=-0.05+0.1\cdot0.6=0.01\). Gene 2: \(d=0.4\), \([-0.10,0.70]\), \(z=-0.10+0.9\cdot0.8=0.62\). Gene 3: \(d=0.1\), \([0.15,0.35]\), \(z=0.15+0.5\cdot0.2=0.25\). Gene 4: \(d=0\), so \(z=0.20\).

7. \(\sum z_i\), and so is \(\mathbf z\) a valid portfolio?

Worked line\(0.01+0.62+0.25+0.20=1.08\neq 1\), so it is invalid: the blend interval reaches outside the parents' box, and the constraint is lost.

8. Repair it by dividing by the sum (all \(z_i\ge0\), so no clipping is needed):

Worked line\(\mathbf z/1.08=(0.0093,\,0.5741,\,0.2315,\,0.1852)\). If a gene had been negative, set it to 0 first, then renormalise.

9. \(F\) of the repaired child:

Worked line\(\boldsymbol\mu\cdot\mathbf z'=0.0007+0.0689+0.0231+0.0111=0.1039\); \(\sum z'^2=0.4175\); \(F=0.1039-0.0418=0.0621\).

written 10. Explain why whole arithmetic crossover always gives a valid portfolio but blend crossover may not, and say when you would still prefer blend. (2 marks)

Model answerWhole arithmetic is a convex combination: \(\sum_i(\alpha P_{1i}+(1-\alpha)P_{2i})=\alpha\cdot1+(1-\alpha)\cdot1=1\), and each gene lies between its parents' values, so it stays \(\ge0\). Blend samples from an interval extended by \(\alpha d_i\) beyond both parents, so the sum and the sign are not preserved and a repair is needed. Prefer blend for exploration (it can leave the parents' box and keeps diversity); whole arithmetic only contracts the population towards its interior (exploitation).

Q2 · Bayes and naive Bayes 25 marks

(a) Gaussian Bayes decision rule 12 marks

The training set has 600 samples of \(\omega_1\) and 400 of \(\omega_2\). The estimates are \(\mathbf m_1=(0,1)\), \(\mathbf C_1=\begin{bmatrix}2&0\\0&1\end{bmatrix}\), \(\mathbf m_2=(2,3)\), \(\mathbf C_2=\begin{bmatrix}1&0.5\\0.5&2\end{bmatrix}\). Classify \(\mathbf x=(1,2)\) with the minimum-error Bayes rule, using \(g_i=\ln P(\omega_i)-\tfrac12\ln|\mathbf C_i|-\tfrac12(\mathbf x-\mathbf m_i)^T\mathbf C_i^{-1}(\mathbf x-\mathbf m_i)\). ML slides 3, pp. 10–15

1. \(|\mathbf C_1|\):

Worked line\(2\cdot1-0=2\). Priors: \(P(\omega_1)=600/1000=0.6\), \(P(\omega_2)=0.4\).

2. Quadratic form \((\mathbf x-\mathbf m_1)^T\mathbf C_1^{-1}(\mathbf x-\mathbf m_1)\):

Hint\(\mathbf x-\mathbf m_1=(1,1)\); the inverse of a diagonal matrix is the reciprocal of each diagonal entry.
Worked line\(\mathbf C_1^{-1}=\mathrm{diag}(0.5,1)\), so \(0.5\cdot1^2+1\cdot1^2=1.5\).

3. \(g_1(\mathbf x)\):

Worked line\(\ln0.6-\tfrac12\ln2-\tfrac12(1.5)=-0.5108-0.3466-0.75=-1.6074\).

4. \(|\mathbf C_2|\):

Worked line\(1\cdot2-0.5^2=1.75\).

5. Quadratic form for class 2:

Hint\(\mathbf C_2^{-1}=\frac{1}{1.75}\begin{bmatrix}2&-0.5\\-0.5&1\end{bmatrix}\), with \(\mathbf x-\mathbf m_2=(-1,-1)\).
Worked line\(\frac{1}{1.75}\big(2(1)+2(-0.5)(1)+1(1)\big)=\frac{2}{1.75}=1.1429\). The signs cancel because both components are \(-1\).

6. \(g_2(\mathbf x)\):

Worked line\(\ln0.4-\tfrac12\ln1.75-\tfrac12(1.1429)=-0.9163-0.2798-0.5714=-1.7675\).

7. Decision (1 or 2):

Worked line\(g_1=-1.6074>g_2=-1.7675\), so \(\omega_1\). Note that \(\mathbf x\) is closer to \(\mathbf m_2\) in Mahalanobis terms (1.14 < 1.5), and the prior and determinant terms flip the decision. Leaving them out gives the wrong class.

written 8. Why can the \((2\pi)^{d/2}\) factor be dropped, and what shape is the boundary when \(\mathbf C_1\ne\mathbf C_2\)? (2 marks)

Model answerIt is the same positive constant for every class, so it does not change which \(g_i\) is largest (after the log it is an additive constant). With unequal covariances the \(\mathbf x^T\mathbf C_i^{-1}\mathbf x\) terms do not cancel, so the boundary is quadratic (a conic). With equal covariances it is linear.

1. Unnormalised score \(P(\text{Flu})\,p(F{=}N\mid\text{Flu})\,p(C{=}Y\mid\text{Flu})\,p(T{=}Y\mid\text{Flu})\):

HintCount within the 6 Flu rows only. Fever = N appears once.

2. The same score for Cold:

written 5. What is "naive" here? What happens if a class had zero patients with Fever = N, and how does the course fix it? (3 marks)

Model answerIt assumes the features are conditionally independent given the class, so \(p(\mathbf x\mid\omega)=\prod_j p(x_j\mid\omega)\) (1). A zero estimate makes the whole product zero whatever the other features say (1). The fix is Laplace (add-one) smoothing: add 1 to each count and add the number of possible values to the denominator (\(+d\), the vocabulary size, in the multinomial case on slide 68) (1).

Q3 · LDA and SVM 25 marks

(a) Fisher LDA from raw samples 13 marks

\(\omega_1:\ (1,2),\ (2,4),\ (3,3)\). \(\omega_2:\ (4,0),\ (6,1),\ (5,2)\). Design the Fisher discriminant \(g(\mathbf x)=\mathbf w^T\mathbf x+w_0\) with \(w_0=-\tfrac12\mathbf w^T(\mathbf m_1+\mathbf m_2)\), then classify \(\mathbf x=(3,2)\) and give its distance to the boundary. ML slides 4, pp. 24–27

1. \(\mathbf m_1\):   \(\mathbf m_2\) is on the next line.

Worked line\(\big(\frac{1+2+3}{3},\frac{2+4+3}{3}\big)=(2,3)\).

2. \(\mathbf m_2\):

Worked line\((5,1)\).

3. \(\mathbf S_1=\sum(\mathbf x-\mathbf m_1)(\mathbf x-\mathbf m_1)^T\): entries \(s_{11}\) \(s_{12}\) \(s_{22}\)

HintThe deviations are \((-1,-1),(0,1),(1,0)\). Sum the outer products, with no division by \(n\).
Worked line\(s_{11}=1+0+1=2\), \(s_{12}=1+0+0=1\), \(s_{22}=1+1+0=2\). \(\mathbf S_2\) has deviations \((-1,-1),(1,0),(0,1)\), which give the same matrix.

4. \(\mathbf S_W\): \(s_{11}\) \(s_{12}\) \(s_{22}\)

Worked line\(\begin{bmatrix}4&2\\2&4\end{bmatrix}\).

5. \(|\mathbf S_W|\):

Worked line\(16-4=12\), so \(\mathbf S_W^{-1}=\frac1{12}\begin{bmatrix}4&-2\\-2&4\end{bmatrix}\).

6. \(\mathbf w=\mathbf S_W^{-1}(\mathbf m_1-\mathbf m_2)\):

Worked line\(\mathbf m_1-\mathbf m_2=(-3,2)\). \(\frac1{12}(4(-3)-2(2),\ -2(-3)+4(2))=\frac1{12}(-16,14)=(-1.3333,\ 1.1667)\).

7. \(w_0\):

Worked line\(\mathbf m_1+\mathbf m_2=(7,4)\); \(\mathbf w^T(7,4)=-9.3333+4.6667=-4.6667\); \(w_0=+2.3333\). Sign check: \(g(\mathbf m_1)=-2.6667+3.5+2.3333=3.1667>0\), so \(g>0\) means \(\omega_1\).

8. \(g(3,2)\):

Worked line\(-4+2.3333+2.3333=0.6667>0\), so \(\omega_1\).

9. Distance of \((3,2)\) to the boundary:

Worked line\(\lVert\mathbf w\rVert=\sqrt{1.7778+1.3611}=1.7717\), so \(0.6667/1.7717=0.3763\). This is small compared with \(g(\mathbf m_1)/\lVert\mathbf w\rVert=1.79\): a low-confidence decision.

written 10. When does Fisher LDA perform best? (2 marks)

Model answerWhen the classes are roughly Gaussian with equal (shared) covariance, so a linear boundary is optimal; when the means are well separated relative to the within-class spread; and with plenty of samples relative to the dimension, so \(\mathbf S_W\) is well estimated and invertible.

(b) Soft-margin SVM 12 marks

A soft-margin linear SVM with \(C=1\) was trained. Its support vectors and multipliers are: \(\mathbf x_A=(3,1),\ d=+1,\ \alpha=0.25\); \(\mathbf x_B=(1,1),\ d=-1,\ \alpha=0.75\); \(\mathbf x_C=(2,0),\ d=-1,\ \alpha=0.5\); \(\mathbf x_D=(2,1.5),\ d=+1,\ \alpha=1\). ML slides 5, pp. 34–40

1. \(\mathbf w=\sum\alpha_i d_i\mathbf x_i\):

HintFirst check \(\sum\alpha_i d_i=0.25-0.75-0.5+1=0\), so the multipliers are consistent.
Worked line\(0.25(3,1)-0.75(1,1)-0.5(2,0)+1(2,1.5)=(0.75-0.75-1+2,\ 0.25-0.75+1.5)=(1,1)\).

2. \(b\), averaged over the correct support vectors:

HintWhich support vectors have \(0<\alpha<C\)? \(\mathbf x_D\) has \(\alpha=C\).
Worked lineUse A, B and C only: \(1-4=-3\), \(-1-2=-3\), \(-1-2=-3\), so \(b=-3\). Trap: including D adds \(1-3.5=-2.5\), and the average becomes \(-2.875\), which is wrong. The lecturer said "if you use all the support vectors you get wrong results".

3. Slack \(\xi_D\) of \(\mathbf x_D\):

Worked line\(g(\mathbf x_D)=2+1.5-3=0.5\), so \(\xi_D=1-d\,g=0.5\): inside the margin but on the correct side. This is consistent with \(\alpha_D=C\).

4. Margin width:

Worked line\(2/\lVert\mathbf w\rVert=2/\sqrt2=1.4142\).

5. \(g(2.5,2)\):

Worked line\(2.5+2-3=1.5>0\), so class \(+1\), beyond the \(+1\) margin.

6. Its distance to the decision boundary:

Worked line\(1.5/\sqrt2=1.0607\), which is more than half the margin (0.7071), so a confident decision.

written 7. Explain the role of \(\xi_i\) and of \(C\) in the soft-margin primal \(\min\ \tfrac12\lVert\mathbf w\rVert^2+C\sum\xi_i\). (3 marks)

Model answer\(\xi_i\ge0\) relaxes the constraint to \(d_i(\mathbf w^T\mathbf x_i+b)\ge1-\xi_i\): \(0<\xi_i\le1\) means inside the margin but correctly classified, and \(\xi_i>1\) means misclassified (1). \(C\) trades margin width against training violations: a large \(C\) gives a narrow margin and few violations, with a risk of overfitting; a small \(C\) gives a wide margin and more violations, which is more robust to noise (1). In the dual, \(C\) becomes the box \(0\le\alpha_i\le C\), and \(\alpha_i=C\) marks the points with \(\xi_i>0\) (1).
