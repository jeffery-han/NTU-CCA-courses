Section A — True / False 10 × 2 = 20 marks

Choose True or False, then write a one-line reason on paper. The reason earns the second mark.

Section B — Calculations

B1. Fisher linear discriminant 10 marks

Two classes in two dimensions:

ClassSamples \((x_1, x_2)\)
\(\omega_1\)(1, 1), (2, 3), (3, 2)
\(\omega_2\)(5, 3), (6, 5), (7, 4)

(a) Find the class means and the within-class scatter matrix \(S_W\). (b) Design the Fisher linear discriminant \(g(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x} + w_0\), with the threshold placed at the midpoint of the projected means, and state the decision rule. Classify \((3.5,\,5)\) and \((4.5,\,0)\). (c) The class means differ in \(x_2\). Explain in one or two sentences why your \(\mathbf{w}\) gives \(x_2\) the weight it does.

B2. Soft-margin SVM: recovering w and b 10 marks

A soft-margin SVM with \(C = 1.5\) was trained, and the solver returned these Lagrange multipliers:

Sample\(\mathbf{x}_i\)\(d_i\)\(\alpha_i\)
1(2, 2)+11.0
2(3, 1)+10.75
3(1, 1)−10.25
4(2, 1)−11.5
5(4, 4)+10
6(0, 0)−10

(a) Verify the equality constraint on the \(\alpha_i\). (b) Compute the weight vector \(\mathbf{w}\). (c) Which samples are support vectors? Which of them lie exactly on the margin? (d) Compute the bias \(b\), stating which samples you use and why. (e) Classify \((3,\,0.5)\) and \((1,\,1.5)\). (f) Where does sample 4 lie relative to the decision boundary, and what is its slack \(\xi_4\)?

B3. Classification tree: choosing the root split 10 marks

A root node holds 16 training samples, 8 from \(\omega_1\) and 8 from \(\omega_2\). Two candidate binary questions:

QuestionLeft child (\(\omega_1\), \(\omega_2\))Right child (\(\omega_1\), \(\omega_2\))
A(6, 2)(2, 6)
B(4, 0)(4, 8)

Compute the impurity drop \(\Delta i\) of each question using (a) entropy impurity, (b) Gini impurity and (c) misclassification impurity, and say which question each measure selects. (d) Comment on the result of (c). Give values to 4 decimal places. You may use \(\log_2 3 \approx 1.585\).

B4. Feature ranking and hierarchical clustering 10 marks (4 + 6)

(a) Rank the two features by Fisher's ratio (variances computed by dividing by the number of samples):

Feature\(\omega_1\)\(\omega_2\)
\(f_1\)1, 2, 34, 5, 6
\(f_2\)0, 4, 86, 10, 14

(b) Five points: A(0,0), B(0,1), C(0,3), D(3,0), E(6,1). Using the Manhattan distance (Minkowski, \(q = 1\)), write the distance matrix. Then run agglomerative hierarchical clustering with single linkage and with complete linkage. For each, give the merge sequence with merge heights, and the two clusters obtained by cutting the dendrogram at two clusters.

Section C — Evaluation 10 marks

A screening test set has 1000 samples, of which 50 are positive. Classifier A predicts negative for every sample. Classifier B gives TP = 40, FN = 10, FP = 60, TN = 890.

(a) Compute accuracy, precision, recall and F1 for both classifiers. (b) Which classifier would you deploy, and why does accuracy mislead here? (c) If you estimated these numbers by k-fold cross-validation, what precaution would you take with the folds, and why?
