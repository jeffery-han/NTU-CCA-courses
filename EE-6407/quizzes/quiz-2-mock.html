Section B — Calculations

B1. Bernoulli Naive Bayes 10 marks

A shop records whether a visitor buys. Features are binary: S = student, H = high income, W = weekend visit.

#SHWClass
1YNYBuy
2YYYBuy
3NYYBuy
4YNNBuy
5YNYBuy
6NYNNo
7NNNNo
8YYNNo

(a) Using a Naive Bayes classifier with maximum-likelihood estimates, classify \(x = (S{=}Y,\ H{=}N,\ W{=}N)\). Give both class scores and the posterior \(P(\text{Buy}\mid x)\). [5]

(b) Classify \(x' = (S{=}N,\ H{=}Y,\ W{=}Y)\) the same way. What problem does this expose? [2]

(c) Repeat (b) with Laplace smoothing \(P(x_j = v \mid \omega) = \dfrac{n_{v,\omega}+1}{N_\omega + 2}\). Priors stay unsmoothed. [3]

B2. Fisher linear discriminant 10 marks

Class \(\omega_1\): \((1,2),\ (2,4),\ (3,3)\).   Class \(\omega_2\): \((5,1),\ (6,3),\ (7,2)\).

(a) Compute \(\mathbf m_1, \mathbf m_2\), the scatter matrices \(S_1, S_2\) and \(S_W\). [3]

(b) Find the Fisher direction \(\mathbf w\) and the threshold \(w_0\) by the midpoint rule. Write \(g(\mathbf x)\) and state the decision rule. [4]

(c) Classify \((4,3)\) and \((4.5,2)\). Give the distance of \((4,3)\) from the decision boundary. [3]

B3. Soft-margin linear SVM 10 marks

A trained soft-margin SVM has \(C = 0.25\). Its support vectors and Lagrange multipliers are:

\(\mathbf x_i\)\(d_i\)\(\alpha_i\)
(3, 3)+10.1875
(4, 2)+10.125
(1, 1)−10.0625
(2, 1)−10.25

(a) Compute \(\mathbf w\). [3]   (b) Compute \(b\), stating which support vectors you use and why. [4]   (c) Classify \((5,0)\). Give the margin width, and the slack \(\xi\) of \((2,1)\). [3]

B4. Decision tree split (Gini) 8 marks

Using Gini impurity: (a) give the root impurity. (b) Evaluate the candidate thresholds 26.5, 33 and 52.5. (c) A binary feature Smoker splits the same 10 samples into Smoker = yes (4Y, 1N) and Smoker = no (2Y, 3N). Which single split should the root use, and what is the impurity drop?

B5. Evaluation metrics 6 marks

A classifier is tested on 200 samples: TP = 40, FN = 10, FP = 8, TN = 142.

(a) Give the accuracy, precision, recall, specificity, F1 and FPR. [3]   (b) What accuracy does a classifier that always predicts “negative” get? What does this show? [3]

B6. Clustering 8 marks

One-dimensional points: A = 1, B = 2, C = 4.5, D = 8, E = 9.5 (Euclidean distance).

(a) Single-linkage HAC: list the merges with their heights. [3]   (b) Do the same with complete linkage, and compare the two dendrograms. [3]   (c) Run k-means with k = 2 from initial centroids 1 and 9.5 until it converges. [2]
