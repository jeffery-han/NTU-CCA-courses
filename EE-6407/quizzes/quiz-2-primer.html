1. Data preparation wk 6

The four data types are nominal, ordinal, interval and ratio. You cannot do arithmetic on nominal data, so fill a missing nominal value with the mode, ideally per class. A box plot uses \(\text{IQR}=Q_3-Q_1\) and fences at \(Q_1-1.5\,\text{IQR}\) and \(Q_3+1.5\,\text{IQR}\). The whisker ends at the nearest actual data value inside the fence. Scaling: min–max \(\frac{x-\min}{\max-\min}\), or z-score \(\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\).

Check: \(Q_1=20,\ Q_3=30\). What is the upper fence?

2. Bayes / Naive Bayes wk 7 · “you must know this”

Decide on the largest \(P(\omega)\,p(x\mid\omega)\); the evidence \(p(x)\) only normalises. Naive Bayes multiplies the per-feature probabilities: \(P(\omega)\prod_j P(x_j\mid\omega)\). For a feature observed as “N”, use \(1-P(Y\mid\omega)\). A zero count wipes out the whole product, so use Laplace smoothing \(\frac{n+1}{N+k}\) (k = number of values; the text example uses vocabulary size). MLE for a Gaussian gives the sample mean and covariance.

Check: Priors are 0.5 / 0.5. For class A the two features have \(P=0.2\) and \(0.5\); for class B, \(0.4\) and \(0.1\). What is the score of the winning class?

3. Fisher LDA wk 8 · “tested in past years”

\(S_i=\sum_{x\in\omega_i}(x-m_i)(x-m_i)^T\), \(S_W=S_1+S_2\), \(\mathbf w=S_W^{-1}(\mathbf m_1-\mathbf m_2)\), \(w_0=-\tfrac12\mathbf w^T(\mathbf m_1+\mathbf m_2)\), \(g(x)=\mathbf w^Tx+w_0\). Decide class 1 if \(g>0\), and check the sign by evaluating \(g(\mathbf m_1)\). Inverse of a 2×2 matrix: \(\frac{1}{ad-bc}\begin{bmatrix}d&-b\\-c&a\end{bmatrix}\). The distance to the boundary is \(g/\lVert\mathbf w\rVert\).

Check: \(S_W=\mathrm{diag}(2,4)\), \(\mathbf m_1-\mathbf m_2=(2,4)\). What is the second component of \(\mathbf w\)?

4. Linear SVM wk 9 · “b calculation often in the exam”

The constraints are \(d_i(\mathbf w^Tx_i+b)\ge 1-\xi_i\), and the margin width is \(2/\lVert\mathbf w\rVert\). From the dual: \(\mathbf w=\sum\alpha_i d_i x_i\) and \(\sum\alpha_i d_i=0\). Compute \(b=d_i-\mathbf w^Tx_i\), averaged only over the SVs with \(0<\alpha_i. SVs with \(\alpha_i=C\) sit inside the margin, so they are excluded. Slack: \(\xi=1-d\,f(x)\).

Check: If \(\mathbf w=(1,2)\), what is the margin width (3 d.p.)?

5. Decision trees wk 10

Gini \(=1-\sum p_k^2\) (maximum 0.5 for two classes). Entropy \(=-\sum p_k\log_2 p_k\) (maximum 1). A split is scored by the size-weighted child impurity; impurity drop = parent − weighted children. For a continuous feature, sort the values and try the midpoints between neighbours. Gain ratio corrects the bias toward splits with many branches. Random forest = bootstrap samples + random feature subsets + majority vote.

Check: What is the Gini of a node with proportions (0.7, 0.3)?

6. Evaluation wk 11

Precision \(=\frac{TP}{TP+FP}\). Recall (sensitivity) \(=\frac{TP}{TP+FN}\). Specificity \(=\frac{TN}{TN+FP}\). FPR \(=1-\)specificity. F1 = harmonic mean of precision and recall. Resampling schemes: holdout, stratified, k-fold, LOOCV (N models), repeated k-fold. ROC plots TPR against FPR as the threshold varies; AUC is the area under it.

Check: TP = 30, FP = 10. What is the precision?

7. Regression wk 11

OLS: \(\theta=(X^TX)^{-1}X^Ty\). Ridge: \(\theta=(X^TX+\lambda I)^{-1}X^Ty\). Lasso uses an L1 penalty and has no closed form. Metrics: MSE, RMSE, MAE, and \(R^2=1-\frac{RSS}{TSS}\). \(R^2\) never decreases when you add a variable, which is why adjusted \(R^2\) penalises extra variables.

Check: RSS = 20, TSS = 100. What is \(R^2\)?

8. Feature selection wk 12

A filter scores subsets with a separability measure: Fisher ratio \(\frac{(m_1-m_2)^2}{\sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2}\), Mahalanobis distance, or scatter-based \(\mathrm{tr}(S_W^{-1}S_B)\). A wrapper puts the classifier in the loop. Search strategies: exhaustive (\(2^d\)), sequential forward selection, sequential backward elimination. Stop at k features or when the score stops improving.

Check: Class means are 0 and 3, variances 1 and 2. What is the Fisher ratio?

9. Clustering wk 12–13

k-means alternates assign-to-nearest and recompute-means until nothing moves. HAC distance between clusters: single linkage = min pairwise distance, complete = max, average = mean. DBSCAN takes eps and MinPts and labels each point core, border or noise; it needs no k and handles non-convex shapes. Silhouette \(s=\frac{b-a}{\max(a,b)}\).

Check: What is the single-linkage distance between \(\{1,2\}\) and \(\{6,9\}\)?
