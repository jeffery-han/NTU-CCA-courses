## Details and specifics (after the table)

### Cross-cutting advice (S5, S6, S1)

- **Front-load required courses in Semester 1.** Otherwise Sem 2's required
  courses collide with the water courses and you're forced to actually study
  (S6).
- **Don't watch lecture replays.** A 3-hour replay you'll never finish; you'll
  check your phone 300 times (S6).
- **Failing is genuinely hard.** For most courses, scoring below 2.5 "takes
  skill." But 4.0–5.0 is very hard in several courses (S6).
- **Exam-cram recipe (S6):** start ~1 month out → skim all PPTs once → do 2–3
  past papers **yourself** (don't read an AI's answers and call it revision) →
  go back to the PPTs → final 2 days, PPT understanding first → in the exam, do
  the front questions first.
- **"拟合" (curve-fitting to past papers) is the dominant strategy** in these
  posts, and several courses (EE6407, EE7207 second half, EE6222) are flagged
  as *fitting-failure risks* where question types shift yearly.
- The **64-series** courses (EE64xx) are popular/hyped; EE6407 specifically is
  called out as "hype ≠ easy" — it needs real time, unlike EE6010 (S2).

### EE6204 — System Analysis (the course you're revising for)

- **Structure:** 2 quizzes + 1 assignment (~8 near-free marks) + final (60%).
- **Part 1** switched to a new lecturer (as of the 25/26 poster). **Quiz 1 was
  basically free marks; Quiz 2 also not hard** — one to two days of drilling is
  enough. **Quiz 3 was converted to an online assignment** due to scheduling —
  effectively free marks (S3).
- **Final:** friendly, **no new question types**, "if you can read the English
  question you'll be fine." Poster scored 4.5/5 on the paper (S3).
- **Content is operations research** — many small topics; **queueing theory
  questions are the twisty ones** (S5). "Final felt like all math problems"
  (S5).
- **Watch question-stem abbreviations.** A DTMC question used the abbreviation
  "NOV"; the poster misread it as "November," lost focus, still got 4.5 after
  the lecturer's discretion (S3). Treat this as: read the stem's defined
  acronyms carefully.
- **Net verdict:** 夯 / 🌟🌟🌟🌟🌟 — one of the safest modules; past-paper
  drilling clears it.

### EE6221 — Robotics & Intelligent Sensing

- Most-recommended Semester 1 course for someone with no robotics background
  (S5).
- **Kinematics** is the early hurdle — it appears in **Quiz 1 and final Q1**.
- **Quiz 2** is conceptual; past quizzes transfer well.
- **Final** is "all in on past papers" — question types barely change year to
  year, ~2 days of cramming passed (S5). Last question is a **Kalman filter**
  problem — high engineering utility, worth learning properly rather than
  fitting (S3).
- Solid linear-algebra basis helps if you want to actually learn it (S3).

### EE6203 — Computer Control Systems

- **Great if you did automation / control as an undergrad**; the course "fails
  people," so without that basis leave extra revision days (S5).
- 3 quizzes (10% + 15% + 15%) + final (60%).
- Poster got ~30/40 on quizzes; final had **noticeable drift from past
  papers** — "didn't finish a single full long question," wrote parts of each,
  landed 3.0 (S5).
- Rumoured attendance sign-ins in the **last 3 lectures** (S3).

### EE6407 — Genetic Algorithms & Machine Learning

- **The GA (Part 1) half's teaching is disconnected from its exam.** Quiz 1
  leans **reading comprehension** — bring a calculator, and don't forget it
  (one poster "陨落"'d without one) (S3).
- **Quiz 2 surprised people:** Gini-index decision tree, peak/plateau theory
  (S3); question style "very unexpected" (S5).
- **Final:** Q1 (CA-style) is often left blank / skippable; new question types
  most years; formulas for the novel questions are easy to under-prepare. One
  poster got 2.25/5 on the paper → **3.0 final after curving** (S3).
- **The ML calculation questions are the drillable part** — past papers reveal
  the patterns (S5).
- **Revise alongside the PPT**, not past papers alone (S2).
- Reception is genuinely split: "褒贬不一" but "still selectable" (S2); "many
  recommend it but I couldn't learn it and didn't score well" (S5).

### EE6483 — AI & Data Mining

- Machine-marked quiz is easy — re-read the PPT twice for a high score (S5).
- Assignments are near-free; project can be built on an open-source repo →
  report (S5).
- **The catch: it's the only 2-hour exam**, half-open-book (1× A4 double
  sided), but the question volume is so high you get no time to consult the
  sheet — poster wrote nonstop start to finish and *barely* finished (S5, S11).
- CS-department courses (this, EE6405) are described as far lighter than the
  comms (EE61xx) courses (S4).

### EE7207 — Neural Networks / Deep Learning

- **Most fractured experience of any course** — 3 lecturers (S7).
- **Weeks 1–7 (Mao):** clear logic, moderate + concise content, "self-study
  max," very comfortable. Exam contribution: ~1 original + 1 partially-changed
  question (S7).
- **Second half (2 lecturers):** too much overlapping, tangled content, unclear
  priorities, "can't absorb it from the slides, pure rote." **Exam types change
  a lot every year, no pattern** — the main risk area (S7).
- Recommended for people pivoting to AI: covers mainstream neural nets + large
  models. First two final questions tend to be past-paper originals; 3 past
  papers + one PPT pass → 3.5 (S3).

### EE6222 / EE7403 / EE6403 / EE6402 (from S8, with a cheat-sheet repo)

- **GitHub:** <https://github.com/bibuzhang-crypto/NTU_EEE_SPML.git> — quiz
  papers and LaTeX/PDF cheat sheets for EE6222, EE6402, EE6403.
- **EE6222:** Part 1 Jiang sir (clear, needs signals + probability), Part 2 a
  younger lecturer named Cheng (loose structure, but conceptual → easy
  self-study). Quiz **fully reformed to concept-only, very easy** ("知识点：马冬梅；
  题目：马什么梅"). Final: Jiang draws a scope covering "a small half" of the
  content; first 3 questions are derivation + calc on top of basic
  understanding; Q4 (Cheng) all concepts.
- **EE7403:** Jiang sir throughout, "EE6222 deepened." Quiz is the **same week**
  as EE6222, similar scope but clearly harder, confusingly similar MCQ options.
  Final tests only a few chapters, proof/derivation-heavy — but *easier to
  write* once you've done the EE6222 exam.
- **EE6402:** "夯爆了" — lectures a bit hard, exam very easy. 2 assignments (2
  calc each) + quiz (3 calc), all easy — **but assignments have a 1-week limit
  and many students score 0 by missing it.** New Dr. Chan shifted Part 1 scope,
  so revise from the slides, not old cheat sheets.
- **EE6403:** two of EEE's best teachers, relaxed lively class, mostly clear
  exam points with a few sneaky ones, normal difficulty. Yap's half has very
  fixed exam templates. Shao's half overlaps ~70% with EE6223 Part 1 and she
  draws a detailed scope → **pair EE6403 with EE6223**.

### EE6223 — Computer Control Networks (S2, 9/10)

- **Part 1 (Shao):** quiz = MCQ + calc + short answer, easy marks. She **draws
  the exam scope precisely**; non-scope material was neither reviewed nor
  tested.
- **Part 2:** graph theory / control theory, extremely math-heavy, "completely
  couldn't follow" — assignment is textbook problems (painful), leaned on
  ChatGPT/Gemini. Exam mostly picks from the quiz plus some proofs; revise
  straight to the questions. Marking "ok."

### EE6301 — Bio / Medical Sensors (S2 5+1/10, S4)

- Half-open-book but "poor experience." Content is understand-only, "messy and
  scattered."
- **2 quizzes, both MCQ, non-trivial — hard to score.** Plus a make-a-PPT
  assignment.
- Part 1 all biology/medical ("no use for an EEE student"); Part 2 has some
  easy circuit content.
- **Past papers have high reference value.** Calc + short answer. Passing isn't
  hard; scoring well is; revising both quiz and exam is a slog; the lecturer
  "挂人" (fails people). "Counts as a pass, being open-book."

### Power Engineering cluster (S9, S10, S12)

- **EE6503:** target course vs. China's "电机学". Circuits background advised.
  Near-universal praise for the lecturer (Chris) → **class is extremely
  competitive** ("很卷很卷很卷"). Fixed exam types; lowest time investment of
  the PE courses; every year's paper is basically the same shape (S9, S10).
- **EE6501 — Power Electronics (S12, ex-TA, got A/A+/A+):**
  - Quiz 1 (20%): single-phase DC-AC full/half bridge — switching sequence,
    waveform sketching, spectra, RMS/power-factor/harmonic-power, series-
    parallel resonance.
  - Quiz 2 (20%): Buck/Boost analysis + control design — on/off timing,
    small-signal model + transfer function, ripple + L/C sizing, compensator
    design to a crossover-frequency / phase-margin spec.
  - Final (60%): 4 long questions, fixed structure — (1) single-phase inverter
    principles + Fourier/harmonic power, (2) three-phase grid-tie inverter: dq
    decoupling, power balance, SPWM harmonics, PI design, SPWM vs SVM, (3)
    DC-DC (one of three types): waveforms, ripple, sizing, cascade, bidirectional,
    Type I/II controller design, (4) AC-DC Boost PFC: topology, control block
    diagram, L/C sizing, interleaved structure, small-signal + controller.
  - **Speed-run:** target Q1/Q3/Q4 — fixed routines. New Part-1 lecturer from
    25 Spring, content largely unchanged. Great for smart-grid / power-
    electronics job hunting. Reference books: 王兆安《电力电子技术》, 邱关源《电路》,
    卢京潮《自动控制原理》, Erickson *Fundamentals of Power Electronics*.
  - **EE6510 / EE6511 / EE6534:** all involve Eddy (clear, follow his scope) and
    Petr (abstract, no scope given — cheat-sheet prep tends to miss). EE6510's
    first lecturer changed to someone who reads slides; self-study Part 1 from
    the PPT, the second (Indian) lecturer is good (S10).

### Semiconductor / ET cluster (S11)

- **EE6601 Advanced Wafer Processing:** 顶尖. Litho / clean / etch / interconnect;
  concepts + small calc; once past-paper-familiar you can near-recite the final.
- **EE6307 Analog IC Design:** 3 lecturers (hard / extremely hard / easy). Learn
  only the 3rd lecturer's part → pass; wing the rest by copying the stem and
  writing plausible formulas. **Fine for a bare pass, terrible if you want a
  high GPA.**
- **EE7602 Integrated Circuit Technology:** the poster's "most regretted"
  choice. Pure humanities-style, "EE6601 plus." Not hard but *enormous* content,
  exam = open a random slide and ask about something on it. Avoid if you have
  any tendency to skip over unfamiliar words when reading.
- **EE6483** (also covered above): 顶尖, but the only 2-hour exam and the
  question volume means the A4 sheet is useless — no time to read it.
